## PART II – THE BAHAMAS DENTAL COUNCIL

### CONTINUATION OF THE BAHAMAS DENTAL COUNCIL

**3.** Continuance of The Bahamas Dental Council.

(1) The body known and existing as The Bahamas Dental Council established under section 3 of the repealed Act, is hereby continued as a body corporate, to be known as The Bahamas Dental Council, with perpetual succession and a common seal, with power to acquire, hold and dispose of land and other property of whatever kind, and to sue and be sued. (2) Notwithstanding subsection (1), the Council may not dispose of any land without the prior approval of the House of Assembly signified by resolution thereof.

(3) The First Schedule shall have effect with respect to the constitution of the Council and its procedures.

(4) The Minister may, by order, amend the First Schedule.

**4.** Functions of Council.

(1) The functions of the Council are to —

(a) govern, discipline, and regulate the professional conduct of dental practitioners and dental auxiliaries;

(b) register and issue licences to practice dentistry;

(c) promote and protect the welfare and interest of registered dental practitioners in harmony with the promotion and protection of the welfare and interest of the general public;

(d) advise registered dental practitioners on standards of professional ethics;

(e) publish annually, a list of all licensed dental practitioners and auxiliaries, inclusive of speciality qualifications approved and accepted by the Council as meriting such listing;

(f) provide certificates of good standing to registered and licensed dental practitioners and auxiliaries upon request in writing to the Council and payment of the prescribed fee by the applicant;

(g) review and publish, at suitable intervals, a list of institutions accepted as accredited for the training of dental practitioners of all levels and the registration and licensing only of persons who qualify from the listed institutions to ensure maintenance of a high standard of persons to perform dental services in The Bahamas;

(h) appoint committees in accordance with section 6;

prescribe courses for continuing dental education requirements for dental practitioners and auxiliaries;

(j) prescribe the number of continuing dental education requirement hours required for renewal of licences for dental practitioners and auxiliaries;

(k) do all such things as are incidental to the objects of this Act or as the Council may think reasonably necessary to achieve the objects or any of them.

**5.** Powers and Duties of the Council

(1) The Council shall, after consultation with the Minister, establish and carry out the policy of the Council.

(2) The Council shall, after consultation with the Bahamas Dental Association, develop —

(a) codes for the guidance of dental practitioners and dental auxiliaries, and

(b) standards relating to the practice of dentistry.

(3) The Council shall ensure that dental practitioners and dental auxiliaries have access to the Principals of Ethics and Professional Conduct. (4) The Council shall have such powers that are necessary to perform its functions under this Act.

**6.** Committees of Council.

(1) The Council shall appoint such committees, as necessary, for the —

(a) promotion of dental education, qualifications and proficiency required for registration and licensing under this Act;

(b) investigation of complaints made against persons registered and licensed under this Act or purporting to be registered or licensed under this Act;

(c) discipline of persons registered or licensed under this Act; and

(d) development of public policy recommendations relating to dentistry.

(2) The composition and procedure of a committee shall be prescribed by regulations.

(3) The Council shall, where the composition and procedure of a committee or subcommittee are not provided for in this Act or the regulations, determine the composition and procedure of the relevant committee.

### FINANCIAL PROVISIONS

**10.** Bank account, funds and resources of the Council.

(1) The Council shall open and maintain a bank account.

(2) The Council shall assign the Chairman, the Registrar and any other member of the Council authorized by the Council in that behalf, as signatories on the bank account, and any two of the three signatories assigned may sign off on any withdrawal from the bank account. (3) The Council shall ensure that all sums received by, or on behalf of the Council, are deposited to the bank account, as soon as practicable, upon receipt.

The funds and resources of the Council shall consist of —

(a) sums of money as may from time to time be provided by Parliament;

sums as may from time to time be accrued to the Council through its own operations;

(c) sums donated to the Council by any person or organization; (d) sums as may be raised by the Council in its own name for the purpose of carrying out its functions where the sums available under paragraphs (a) through (c) are insufficient; and

(e) sums as may lawfully be paid or vested in the Council whether or not in respect of any matter incidental to its functions.

**11.** Accounts and audit.

(1) The accounts of the Council shall be audited annually by an auditor appointed in each year by the Council, and the report of the audit so made, including a full financial statement of the affairs of the Council, shall be presented to the Council at the next Annual General Meeting.

(2) A copy of the audited financial statements shall be kept at the office of the Council and shall at all reasonable times be open to inspection.

### III – REGISTRATION, LICENSING AND PERMITS

### REGISTERS

**12.** Register of practitioners and auxiliaries.

(1) The Registrar shall keep and maintain, in such form as the Council may determine, a general register of dental practitioners and auxiliaries, subdivided into the following sub-registers —

(a) Register of General Dentists;

(b) Register of Dental Specialists;

(c) Register of Dental Nurses;

(d) Register of Dental Hygienists;

(e) Register of Dental Technicians;

(f) Register of Limited Dental Practitioners;

(g) Register of Dental Therapists;

(h) Register of Dental Assistants;

(i) Register of Dental Interns,

in which the particulars specified in subsection (2), shall be entered in respect of each registrant.

For the purposes of subsection (1), the particulars to be entered in respect of each registrant shall include —

(a) full name;

(b) date of birth;

(c) certified copies of the registrant's academic and professional qualifications;

(d) a copy of any licence issued to the registrant under this Act; and

(e) other records required to be kept in accordance with this Act.

**13.** Alteration to registers.

*The*

Registrar shall in relation to any register —

(a) remove, suspend, or enter any condition, restriction or limitation against, any entry as the Council may direct in writing;

(b) correct in accordance with the Council's written directions, any entry, which the Council considers incorrectly made;

(c) restore as directed by the Council in writing, any entry that has been removed;

(d) remove the name of any —

(i) deceased person; or

(ii) person who is no longer qualified to conduct dental services in The Bahamas; and

(e) make, from time to time, any necessary alterations in any of the particulars contained in the registers.

**14.** Publication of list of licensees.

The Registrar shall, on or before 30 th day of April in each year, publish in (1)

the Gazette, an annual list of all licensees.

(2) The General Register and the Register of licensees shall be open to inspection by any member of the public during normal business hours and such person may, on payment of the prescribed fee, obtain a copy of any part of the register.

(3) A copy of the annual list published pursuant to subsection (1), shall be prima facie evidence that —

(a) a person included in the list is licensed under this Act; and

(b) a person whose name is absent from the list is not licensed under this Act.

(4) Without prejudice to subsection (3), a written confirmation by the Registrar that a person is licensed shall be evidence that such person is licensed under this Act.

### REGISTRATION AND LICENSING OF PRACTITIONERS AND AUXILIARIES

**15.** Mandatory registration and licensing.

(1) No person shall engage in the practice of dentistry or provide dental services unless he is registered and licensed under this Act.

(2) A person who is not registered in accordance with this Act shall —

(a) be deemed not to be a dental practitioner or dental auxiliary; and

(b) not be eligible for the grant of a licence under this Act.

**16.** Registration of dental practitioners and auxiliaries.

(1) A person who is desirous of being registered as a dental practitioner or dental auxiliary, and is eligible for registration shall apply to the Council. (2) An application under subsection (1) shall be in Form 1A set out in the Second Schedule, and shall be accompanied by —

(a) the prescribed application fee;

(b) the prescribed registration fee; and

(c) certified copies of relevant certificates.

(3) Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Council —

(a) may, on such terms and conditions as it deems fit, grant registration in the General Register to an applicant who satisfies the requirements of section 18;

(b) shall refuse the application of a person who does not satisfy the requirements for registration.

(4) An application made under subsection (1) by a person who is not either —

(a) a public officer; or

(b) a citizen of The Bahamas,

shall not be approved and the person shall not be registered without the concurrence of the Minister responsible for Immigration.

**17.** Registration of a dental practitioner as a specialist practitioner. (1) For the purposes of this Act, the Council recognizes as a specialist, the areas of dental practice specified in the Third Schedule, and in relation to each speciality —

(a) the institution or body whose certification shall be accepted as proof of qualification; and

the minimum duration of relevant postgraduate training and practice required as specified in the Third Schedule.

A dental practitioner shall be eligible to be registered as a specialist in the relevant sub-register, if that dental practitioner satisfies the Council that he has obtained the required qualifications and training for that speciality from an institution listed in Part I of the Third Schedule.

(3) The Council may require a dental practitioner who desires to be registered as a specialist, pursuant to subsection (2) to submit to a review by the Education Committee, if the Council deems it necessary.

(4) An application for registration as a specialist shall be made to the Council in Form 1A set out in the Second Schedule, and shall be accompanied by —

(a) the prescribed application fee;

(b) the prescribed registration fee; and

(c) certified copies of relevant certificates.

(5) If the Council receives an application referred to in subsection (4) and is satisfied that an applicant is qualified to be registered as a specialist, the Council shall, upon the applicant making payment of the prescribed fee, grant to the applicant a certificate of registration in Form 2A specified in the Second Schedule.

(6) The Council shall register, in the register to be called the specialist register, a dental practitioner who possesses the qualification relating to a speciality specified in Part I of the Second Schedule.

(7) The specialist register shall include the speciality for which each specialist named is registered.

(8) A general dentist who is not registered as a specialist and who represents that he is entitled to engage in the practice as a specialist is guilty of professional misconduct.

**18.** Eligibility for registration.

(1) A person shall be eligible for registration as a dental practitioner or auxiliary if he —

(a) is a citizen or permanent resident of The Bahamas;

(b) can read, write, speak and understand the English Language;

(c) has passed a Board Examination approved by the Council; and

(d) has met the prescribed academic qualification requirements for registration in a specific sub-register.

Notwithstanding subsection (1)(a), a person who is not a citizen or a permanent resident of The Bahamas may be eligible for registration where

the Council determines that he is qualified to provide specific dental services in The Bahamas.

For the purposes of subsection (2), the Council in assessing the need for specific dental services, shall take into account relevant considerations including, but not limited to, the number of dental practitioners currently providing such services in The Bahamas and the number of Bahamians currently enrolled in training programs with respect to such services.

**19.** Qualifications.

*For*

the purposes of sections 18, a person satisfies the academic qualification requirements for registration as a —

(a) dental practitioner if —

(i) he holds a degree, certificate or other forms of qualification granted by a university, college or other institutions of learning recognised by the Council as a body empowered to confer authority to practice dentistry by the law of the country or place where it is granted; and

(ii) he provides evidence of dental training for an average of thirty-five hours per week for not less than one year;

(b) a dental auxiliary, if he has completed a course of training approved by the Council.

**20.** Limited registration.

(1) The Council may grant a form of limited registration referred to in subsection (2) for the purposes of enabling an applicant to —

(a) become eligible for full registration;

(b) teach or to undertake research or study within The Bahamas;

(c) provide dental services in the public health system.

(2) Limited registration may be granted to an applicant —

(a) temporarily, for a period not exceeding six months where that applicant satisfies the Council that he is temporarily in The Bahamas and would otherwise qualify for registration under section 18 by virtue of his qualifications to practice dentistry or perform dental services in his country or territory;

(b) provisionally, for a period not exceeding one year, where that applicant satisfies the Council that he is enrolled in —

(i) an internship approved by the Council; or

(ii) a general practice residency program in The Bahamas for the period of the internship;

intermediately, for a period not exceeding one year, where the Council has determined that the applicant —

has academic or practical training deemed to be deficient in some area;

(ii) has not passed some aspect of the required board examination and, in the opinion of the Council, would benefit from additional practical experience and training under specified tutelage prescribed by the Council;

(iii) being a registered practitioner, has a standard of proficiency deemed to be unsatisfactory and requires additional training before being returned to the general register; and (d) specially, for a period not exceeding one year, where the applicant satisfies the Council that he is qualified to practice dentistry or perform dental services in any country or territory and he is — (i) doing special work in the field of public health or research and is sponsored in such work by the World Health Organisation, the Pan American Health Organisation or such other organisation or body as the Minister, after consultation with the Council, approves; or

(ii) employed on a full time basis in the public service to practice dentistry or to perform dental services.

(3) The Council may, in granting limited registration, impose on the registrant such conditions, restrictions and limitations as the Council thinks fit, including but not limited to —

(a) restricting the places or times at which the registrant may provide dental services;

(b) limiting the kind of dental services that the registrant may provide;

(c) limiting the period during which the registration will have effect;

(d) requiring that a practising dentist licensed under this Act supervise the registrant in the provision of dental services.

**21.** Certificate of registration.

*The*

Council shall, on the approval of an application for registration under section 16, direct the Registrar to issue a certificate of registration in Form 2A as specified in the Second Schedule.

**22.** Annual licence required to practise dentistry.

(1) A registrant who desires to practise as a dental practitioner or auxiliary in any year shall, prior to the 31 st day of January in that year, apply for a licence to practise (hereinafter referred to as “annual licence”).

Every annual licence granted by the Council and issued by the Registrar shall —

(a) be recorded in the register;

(b) be valid from the date it is specified to take effect;

(c) expire on the 31 st day of December in each year; and

(d) be renewable annually upon the application of the holder of the licence and the payment of the prescribed fee not later than the 31 st day of January in each year.

(3) Any person who does not renew his licence by the 31 st day of January in that year, shall pay to Registrar the late fee prescribed in regulations.

**23.** Eligibility for licence.

(1) Every registered dental practitioner or auxiliary shall be eligible for a licence to practise if he —

(a) is registered in the relevant sub-register with respect to the dental service for which he has applied to be licensed;

(b) has satisfied the requisite continuing dental education requirements as specified in the Fourth Schedule;

(c) has not been disqualified or suspended from practising as a dental practitioner or dental auxiliary within or outside The Bahamas;

(d) is medically fit to provide the dental services authorised by registration on the relevant sub-register; and

(e) has paid to the Registrar the prescribed fee.

(2) Every person granted a licence shall display his licence in a prominent place in his place of practice.

(3) For the purposes of subsection (1)(a) “registered” means current registration which has not been suspended or revoked.

**24.** Limited licence.

(1) A person granted limited registration may apply to the Council for a limited licence provided that the limited licence shall —

(a) be valid from the date it is specified to take effect on;

(b) expire on or before the expiration of the holder's limited registration; and

(c) be renewable upon the application of the holder of the licence only where the holder continues to be registered.

The Council may approve an application for a limited licence and grant the limited licence to an applicant who —

(a) has a valid limited registration in accordance with section 20; has not been disqualified or suspended from practising as a dental practitioner or dental auxiliary within or outside The Bahamas; and (c) where applicable, has obtained, a valid work permit from the Minister responsible for Immigration.

**25.** Continuing duty of licensee.

Every licensee shall have a continuing duty to —

(a) inform the Registrar of any changes of address;

(b) meet all continuing dental education requirements as may be prescribed; and

(c) adhere to the principles and standards for professional conduct — (i) as outlined in the Principals of Ethics and Professional Conduct document; or

(ii) in Regulations prescribed by the Minister.

**26.** Issue of licence.

(1) The Registrar, on behalf of the Council, shall issue to every registrant whose application for a licence has been approved by the Council, a licence, in Form 2B as specified in the Second Schedule.

(2) A licence shall be granted —

(a) for a period of one year and shall be renewable annually; or

(b) in the case of an applicant with limited registration, for a period of time commensurate with the duration of his limited certificate of registration.

(3) The Registrar may provide a copy of a licence issued under subsection (1) for a prescribed fee.

**27.** Renewal of licence.

(1) A dental practitioner or dental auxiliary registered under section 16 shall, prior to the 31 st December, apply to the Council for a renewal of licence in Form 1B as specified in the Second Schedule accompanied by the prescribed fees.

(2) On the application for a renewal of licence, the Council may renew the licence of a dental practitioner or dental auxiliary if the Council is satisfied that he continues to meet the requirements under this Act, and that he —

(a) has participated in postgraduate training, or in symposia or other approved activities that provide information or training relating to updated techniques in the dental profession;

has been exposed to updated techniques or procedures in the practice of dentistry;

has completed the prescribed contact hours of continuing dental education for renewal of licence;

(d) in relevant cases, has provided proof of a valid work permit; and (e) has made payment of the prescribed fee.

**28.** Display, copies of certificates and licences, etc.

Every holder of a certificate of registration or licence issued under this Act shall display such certificate or licence in a prominent position visible to patients in his place of practice.

**29.** Continuing Dental Education.

(1) Every calendar year, a dental practitioner or dental auxiliary shall undertake continuing dental education for the minimum number of hours specified in the Fourth Schedule.

(2) The continuing dental education courses and activities shall meet the criteria specified in the Fourth Schedule.

**30.** Refusal or suspension of licence.

(1) The Council may refuse to renew a licence where a holder of a licence has —

(a) failed to satisfy any prescribed continuing dental education requirement;

(b) failed to pay the prescribed licence fee;

(c) been suspended and the period of suspension continues through the time for renewal.

(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1)(a), the Council may suspend a licence until the holder of that licence meets the prescribed continuing dental education requirements.

(3) The Council shall not renew the licence of an applicant under subsection (1), unless he completes the required number of continuing dental education requirement hours prescribed by the Council in the Fourth Schedule.

### SPECIAL PERMITS

Interpretation for the purpose of sections 31 and 32.

For the purposes of sections 31 and 32 —

“conscious sedation” means a minimally depressed level of consciousness produced by a pharmacological method, a non pharmacological method, or a combination of both, in which a patient retains the ability to maintain a patent airway independently and to respond purposefully to physical stimulation or verbal command;

“deep sedation” means a controlled pharmacologically induced state of depressed consciousness where the patient is not easily aroused and which may be accompanied by a partial loss of protective reflexes including the ability to maintain a patent airway independently or respond purposefully to physical stimulation or verbal command, or both; and

“nitrous oxide” means the diminution or elimination of pain in the conscious patient through the use of nitrous oxide and oxygen.

**32.** Local anaesthesia permit.

(1) A dental hygienist who is desirous of obtaining a permit to administer local anaesthesia shall apply to the Council in writing in Form 3 of the Second Schedule.

(2) The Council may grant a local anaesthesia permit to a dental hygienist, upon payment of a one time prescribed fee, where that hygienist —

(a) hold a current dental licence in good standing;

(b) hold a current certificate in Basic Life Support (BLS); and

(c) provides the Council with documentation, evidencing that he undertook a training program which included didactic and clinical courses in the administration of local anaesthesia.

(3) A local anaesthesia permit, granted by the Council under subsection (2), shall —

(a) be valid from the date it is specified to take effect;

(b) expire on the 31 st day of December in each year; and

(c) be renewable annually upon the application of the holder of the permit not later than the 31 st day of January in each year. (4) The Registrar shall issue a special permit certificate for local anaesthesia, in Form 4 of the Second Schedule for every permit granted by the Council under this section.

**33.** Deep sedation permit.

(1) A dentist who is desirous of providing deep sedation, to adult and paediatric patients shall apply to the Council in writing in Form 3 of the Second Schedule , for a deep sedation permit.

The Council may grant a sedation permit to a dentist who —

(a) has a current dental licence in good standing;

(b) has a current certificate in Basic Life Support (BLS);

(c) provides the Council with documentation, evidencing that he undertook a training program (specialist) which included the didactic and clinical training in this area;

(d) provides Council with current malpractice insurance certificate;

(e) has a valid certification in advanced cardiovascular life support issued by an approved and recognised institution;

(f) provides the Council with documentation evidencing that he undertook a training program which included didactic and clinical training in —

(i) the provision of deep sedation or conscious sedation; or

(ii) the administration of nitrous oxide and oxygen.

(3) A deep sedation permit, granted by the Council under subsection (2), shall —

(a) be valid from the date it is specified to take effect;

(b) expire on the 31 st day of December in each year; and

(c) be renewable annually upon the application of the holder of the permit and the payment of the prescribed fee, not later than the 31 st day of January in each year.

(4) The Registrar shall issue a special permit certificate for deep sedation, in Form 4 of the Second Schedule for every permit granted by the Council under this section.

## PART IV – MISCELLANEOUS

### DISCIPLINE

**34.** Complaints.

(1) Any member of the public may make a complaint against a dental practitioner or dental auxiliary.

(2) The Council may, of its own initiative, institute a complaint against any person contravening this Act.

(3) The procedures in relation to the lodging of and processing of complaints shall be prescribed in regulations.

Professional misconduct.

If any person registered under this Act is found, upon enquiry by the Council to —

(a) be suffering from any illness rendering him unfit to practice dentistry or perform satisfactorily the dental services in respect of which he is registered;

(b) be guilty of dishonesty, negligence or incompetence in the performance of his functions as a dentist, or of serious professional misconduct; or

(c) have procured his registration under this Act as a result of any misleading, false or fraudulent representation, the Council may, if it thinks fit, either suspend his licence for a period not exceeding one year or direct the Registrar to remove his name from the register as the case may require.

(2) For the purposes of paragraph (b) of subsection (1), “serious professional misconduct” includes any act or thing done by a person registered under this Act that is contrary to the generally recognised duty and responsibility of such a person to his patient or the failure to do any act or thing with respect to a patient in accordance with such duty and responsibility, and without limiting the generality of the foregoing includes —

(a) improper conduct or association with a patient;

(b) any form of advertising, canvassing or promoting directly for the purpose of obtaining patients or promoting his own professional advantage;

(c) wilful or deliberate betrayal of a professional confidence;

(d) abandonment of a patient in danger without sufficient cause and without allowing the patient sufficient opportunity to retain the services of another dentist or dental auxiliary;

(e) knowingly giving a certificate with respect to any matter relating to dentistry or dental services which the dentist or dental auxiliary knows or ought to know is untrue, misleading or otherwise improper;

(f) the division with any person who is not a partner or assistant of any fees or profits resulting from the taking of advice or from other dental services without the patient’s knowledge or consent;

(g) the abuse of intoxicating liquor or drugs;

(h) the impersonation of another dentist or dental auxiliary;

(i) association with unqualified or unregistered persons whereby such persons are enabled to practise dentistry or perform dental services;

(j) the holding out directly or indirectly by a dentist or dental auxiliary to the public that he is specially qualified in any particular branch of dentistry or dental services unless he has taken a special course in that branch and such special qualification has been registered in accordance with the provisions of this Act;

(k) any wilful or negligent misrepresentation as to the curative efficacy possessed by a drug or any other substance, whether inherently or by administration or application thereof;

(l) knowingly practising dentistry, performing dental services or treating a patient other than in a case of emergency while suffering from a mental or physical condition or while under the influence of alcohol or drugs to such an extent as to constitute a danger to the public or a patient;

(m) the doing of or failure to do any act or thing in connection with his professional practice, which is in the opinion of the Council unprofessional or discreditable; and

(n) conviction of an indictable offence.

(3) For the purposes of paragraph (c) of subsection (2) any disclosure of confidential information by a person registered under this Act pertaining any patient shall not be deemed to be wilful or deliberate where such disclosure is required by any law for the treatment of that patient or for the protection of others against serious injury.

(4) For the purposes of this Act, a —

(a) dental practitioner who engages in or represents that he is entitled to engage in the practice of a speciality without being so registered and licensed;

(b) dental practitioner or dental auxiliary whose advertisement contravenes the restrictions on advertisement under this Act; (c) dental practitioner or dental auxiliary who obstructs an inspector in the good faith performance of his functions under this Act; (d) dental practitioner or dental auxiliary who acts in a manner which impedes the function of the Council or a committee, commits an act of professional misconduct.

**36.** Appeals.

(1) Subject to this section, a person who is aggrieved by —

(a) a decision of the Council in exercise of its powers in relation to a complaint or disciplinary action; or

the enforcement of any order of a Council Committee,

may within twenty-one days after the receipt of the notice of the decision or enforcement of the order appeal to the Supreme Court against that decision or order by serving on the Council a notice in the prescribed form of intention to appeal and the grounds of appeal signed by his counsel and attorney or by himself.

(2) The Council shall, upon receiving the notice of appeal, transmit to the Supreme Court a copy of its decision and all papers relating to the appeal. (3) No further appeal shall lie from the decision of the Supreme Court with respect to an appeal under this section.

(4) The Council may, pending an appeal under this section, on the application of the aggrieved person suspend the operation of the relevant decision or enforcement of the order until the determination of the appeal by the Supreme Court.

(5) No appeal shall lie under this section against refusal by the Council of an application for registration in the General Register in any case in which such refusal to register is due to the applicant's failure to satisfy the Council that he is qualified to be registered.

(6) The Supreme Court may, upon the hearing of an appeal under this section —

(a) make such order, including an order for costs, as it thinks just; and

(b) exercise any power which the Council might have exercised.

**37.** Restoration to register.

(1) If a dental practitioner or dental auxiliary is struck off the register in accordance with section 13(a), he may make application to be restored to the register in accordance with the prescribed procedure.

(2) If the Council is satisfied that the purpose the dental practitioner or dental auxiliary was struck off the register no longer exists or has been rectified, the Council may direct the Registrar to restore the dental practitioner or dental auxiliary to the register upon payment of the prescribed fee by the dental practitioner or dental auxiliary.

### ADVERTISING

**38.** Restriction on the use of professional standing.

*A used*

dental practitioner or specialist shall not permit his professional standing to be for the purpose of advertising any particular product, service, or organisation.

### OFFENCES

Offences by unregistered or unlicensed persons.

(1) Any person who, not being registered and licensed in accordance with this Act, and who —

(a) performs dental services;

(b) practices as a dental practitioner; or

(c) otherwise engages in the practice of dentistry, commits an offence.

(2) Any person who —

(a) is not registered under this Act and assumes or uses any name, title, addition or description implying that he is entitled to be recognised as a person qualified to practice as a dental practitioner; or

(b) is not licensed under this Act, advertises or holds himself out as a person authorised to practice as a dental practitioner, commits an offence.

(3) Any person who procures or attempts to procure the registration or licensing of himself or any other person as a dental practitioner by —

(a) making or producing, or causing to be made or produced, any fraudulent representation or declaration either verbally, in writing or otherwise; or

(b) any other dishonest conduct or false representation of a material particular,

commits an offence.

(4) A person who commits an offence under this section shall, on summary conviction be liable —

(a) for a first offence, to a fine not exceeding ten thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months or to both such fine and imprisonment; and

(b) for a subsequent offence, to a fine not exceeding twenty thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding twelve months or to both such fine and imprisonment.

**40.** Provision of unauthorised dental services by licensee.

Any practitioner licensed under this Act who practices dentistry or performs dental services other than in accordance with the terms of his licence commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction —

(a) for a first offence, to a fine not exceeding five thousand dollars and to a further fine not exceeding five hundred dollars for every day or part of a day during which such offence continues; and

for a subsequent offence, in addition to paragraph (a), to suspension of his licence for a period not exceeding twelve months.

**41.** Possession or control of dental health appliances or supplies.

(1) No person shall have in his possession or control, dental health appliances, supplies or other devices unless he is —

(a) registered under this Act;

(b) in the business of supplying dental health appliances, supplies and other devices;

(c) in possession or control of the dental health appliance, supply or device for his own personal use;

(d) a person permitted by the Council to have such possession or control;

(e) a medical doctor;

(f) a veterinarian who uses similar instruments or equipment. (2) A person who contravenes subsection (1), commits an offence and shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding ten thousand dollars or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months or both such fine and imprisonment.

(3) For the purposes of subsection (1), possession or control of dental health appliances, supplies or other devices by a person is prima facie evidence that the possession or control is for the purpose of practising as a dental practitioner.

(4) A magistrate who is satisfied, upon information on oath, that there are reasonable grounds for believing that there are in any building or premises, dental health appliances, supplies or other devices which will afford evidence as to the commission of an offence under this section, may issue a warrant under his hand authorising any police officer named in the warrant —

(a) to enter and search the building or premises; and

(b) to seize such dental health appliances, supplies or other devices. (5) The provisions of section 70 of the Criminal Procedure Code (Ch. 91) shall apply to a search warrant issued pursuant to this section.

### GENERAL

Notice of change of address.

Where a dental practitioner or specialist has changed the physical address of practice, he may post a sign outside the premises of the former office for a period of three months.

**43.** Performance of dental services by a dental student.

Nothing in this Act shall prevent any clinical level dental student or dental intern from performing dental services, which are —

(a) approved by the faculty of the relevant institution, organisation or body as a part of a course of instruction in dentistry; and

(b) performed under the direct supervision and control of a dentist registered and licensed under this Act.

**44.** Confidentiality.

(1) A member of the Council, a committee, the Registrar or any person acting in their behalf shall preserve confidentiality with regard to the information obtained by him in the course of his duties, employment, inquiry or investigation under this Act, and shall not disclose such information to any other person except —

(a) as may be required in connection with the administration of, or any proceedings under this Act and the regulations;

(b) to his counsel and attorney; or

(c) with the consent of the person to whom the information relates. (2) No person to whom subsection (1), applies shall be required to give testimony in any civil suit or proceeding with regard to information obtained by him in the course of his duties, employment, inquiry or investigation except —

(a) in a proceeding under this Act; or

(b) upon the order of the Supreme Court.

**45.** Exemptions.

Nothing in this Act shall extend or be construed to extend to prejudice or in any way affect —

(a) the practice by any person of any profession or occupation under and in accordance with any enactment authorising such practice;

(b) the practice of dentistry or provision of dental services by foreign armed forces restricted to the members of their armed forces their

families and households in accordance with any agreement entered into with the Government; or

the administration of dental aid in all cases of necessity provided that there is no hope of reward, hire, fee, gain, or other advantage or benefit to the person giving the aid.

**46.** Non-recovery of fees.

### CONSTITUTION AND PROCEDURE OF THE COUNCIL

**1.** Composition.

(1) The Council shall consist of seven (7) members of whom —

(a) one shall be the Chief Medical Officer ex officio;

(b) one shall be the Director of Oral Health in the Ministry of Health;

(c) one shall be a dentist appointed by the Minister acting on his own discretion;

(d) two shall be individuals eligible to be registered as dentists under this Act appointed by the Minister in accordance with the advice of The Bahamas Dental Association;

(e) one shall be an individual eligible to be registered as a dental auxiliary under this Act appointed by the Minister in accordance with the advice of the Association representing Dental Auxiliaries;

(f) one shall be an individual eligible to be registered under this Act appointed by the Minister acting at his own discretion. (2) No dental practitioner shall be appointed to the Council save and except he has a minimum of seven years actual experience in the practice of dentistry in The Bahamas which period need not be an unbroken period of seven years.

(3) In exercising his powers of appointment under this paragraph, the Minister shall ensure that after the initial constitution of the Council the members of the Council shall not consist entirely of persons previously appointed to membership of the Council at the same time.

**2.** Tenure of Office.

(1) Subject to the provisions of this Schedule, a member of the Council other than an ex officio member shall hold office for a term not exceeding three years and shall be eligible for reappointment. (2) No member of the Council, other than an ex officio member, shall hold office for more than two consecutive terms.

Chairman.

The Minister shall appoint one of the members of the Council to be the Chairman for a term not exceeding three years.

The Council shall elect from among its members a deputy chairman for a term not exceeding three years and such person shall be eligible for re– election.

(3) The Chairman shall preside at all meetings of the Council at which he is present and, in the case of the Chairman's absence from any meeting, the deputy chairman shall preside.

**4.** Resignation.

(1) Any member of the Council, other than the Chairman, may at any time resign his office by instrument in writing addressed to the Minister and transmitted through the Chairman, and, from the date of receipt by the Minister of such instrument such member shall cease to be a member of the Council.

(2) The Chairman may at any time resign his office by instrument in writing addressed to the Minister and from the date of receipt by the Minister of such instrument such Chairman shall cease to be Chairman of the Council.

**5.** Dismissal.

(1) The Minister may at any time revoke the appointment of any member of the Council.

(2) Prior to the revocation of the appointment of a member appointed under sub-sub-paragraphs (d), (e) and (f) of paragraph 1(1), the Minister shall consult with the relevant Association about the revocation.

(3) Any member other than an ex officio member who fails to attend three consecutive meetings of the Council without written excuse to the Chairman shall cease to be a member of the Council.

**6.** Vacancies and acting appointments.

*If shall shall,*

*from*

any vacancy occurs in the membership of the Council, such vacancy be filled by the appointment by the Minister of another member who subject to the provisions of this Schedule, hold office for the remainder of the period for which the previous member was appointed, so, however, that such appointment shall be made in the same manner and the same category of persons, if any, as the member replaced.

**7.** Gazetting of appointments.

(1) The names of all members of the Council as first constituted and every change in membership shall be published in the Gazette. (2) The Council shall elect from among its members a deputy chairman for a term not exceeding three years and such person shall be eligible for re– election.

The Chairman shall preside at all meetings of the Council at which he is present and, in the case of the Chairman's absence from any meeting, the deputy chairman shall preside.

**8.** Interest of member.

(1) A member who has a direct or indirect pecuniary interest in a matter being considered or about to be considered by the Council shall, as soon as possible after the relevant facts have come to his knowledge, disclose the nature of his interest at a meeting of the Council.

(2) A disclosure under sub-paragraph (1) shall be recorded in the minutes of the meeting of the Council and that member shall not, unless the Council otherwise determines—

(a) be present during any deliberation of the Council with respect to that matter, or

(b) take part in any decision of the Council with respect to that matter.

(3) The Council, when making a determination under sub-paragraph (2), shall ensure that the member who had made the disclosure under sub-paragraph (1) shall not—

(a) be present during any deliberation of the Council for the purpose of making the determination; or

(b) take part in the making by the Council of the determination.

**9.** Procedures.

(1) The Council shall meet at least once each month or as often as may be necessary for the transaction of its business. The meetings shall be held at places and times as the Council may determine. (2) The Chairman —

(a) may at any time call a special meeting of the Council; and

(b) shall, within seven days of receipt of a written request addressed to him by any four members of the Council, call a special meeting of the Council.

(3) If both the Chairman and deputy chairman are absent the members present constituting a quorum shall elect a Chairman from among their number to preside at that meeting.

(4) Five (5) members of the Council shall form a quorum.

(5) The decisions of the Council shall be by a majority of votes and, in addition to an original vote, the Chairman or other person presiding at the meeting shall have a casting vote in any case in which the voting is equal.

Minutes in proper form of each meeting shall be kept by the Council.

Subject to the provisions of this Schedule the Council may regulate its own proceedings.

**10.** Validity of decisions of Council.

No decision taken by the Council, or act done by or under the authority of the Council, by a majority vote of the persons who at the time were entitled to act as members shall be invalid by reason that —

(a) the full number of members for which provision is made in paragraph 1 of this Schedule were not appointed or there was a vacancy or vacancies amongst such members, or

(b) a disqualified person acted as a member of the Council at the time the decision was taken or the act was done or authorized.

**11.** Protection of members.

(1) No member of the Council shall be personally liable for any act or default of the Council done or omitted to be done in good faith in the course of the operations of the Council.

(2) Where any member of the Council is exempt from liability by reason only of the provisions of this paragraph, the Council shall be liable to the extent that it would be if the member was a servant or agent of the Council.

**12.** Remuneration of members.

There shall be paid from the funds of the Council to the Chairman and other members of the Council such remuneration whether by way of honorarium, salary or fees, and such allowances as the Minister may determine.

## FOURTH SCHEDULE (section 29)

### CONTINUING DENTAL EDUCATION REQUIREMENTS

*Credit hour*

requirements for continuing dental education (“CDE”) requirements:

(1) All applicants for a licence shall complete the following CDE credit hours during the preceding one year period —

(a) General Dentists and Dental Specialists - 15 hours;

(b) Dental Hygienists - 10 hours;

(c) Dental Therapists – 10 hours;

(d) Dental Nurses - 10 hours.

(2) Continuing dental education subject areas.

The required credit hours shall be completed in subjects which contribute directly to the clinical competence of practitioners. Credit will NOT be awarded for non-clinical courses.

Acceptable subject areas include:

(a) Clinical and technical subjects;

(b) Diagnosis and treatment of oral diseases;

(c) Medical and scientific subjects;

(d) Infection control;

(e) Emergency procedures excluding CPR;

(f) Jurisprudence for the practice of dentistry;

(g) Ethics for the practice of dentistry; and

(h) Recognition of Domestic Abuse.

(3) Advanced Dental Hygienist Requirement.

A dental hygienist who holds a local anaesthesia permit shall complete three (3) of the ten (10) CDE hours in courses related to the administration of local anaesthesia including pharmacology or other related topics. (4) Reporting of continuing dental education credit hours.

(a) The applicant shall provide the information concerning the required hours on the CDE application provided by the Dental Council. CDE certificates may be submitted to the Council’s office as the applicant accrues credit.

(b) The applicant shall provide a copy of the documentation supporting the completion of the credit hours.

Acceptable documentation consists of any one of the following: A CDE certificate, or sponsor-generated print out.

(ii) A certified transcript of courses taken for credit in an accredited institution

(iii) Evidence of publication for published articles, books or CDE programs.

(iv) Evidence obtained from the program sponsor of having been an instructor, including an agenda.

(d) The responsibility for documenting the CDE requirements rests with the applicant. The documentation shall be maintained for four

(4) years after the completion of the programs.

(e) Falsification of CDE information (deceptive, misleading, or fraudulent representations) is a violation of the process and will render fraudulent documents unacceptable.

(f) Failure to comply with (4)(c) may result in disciplinary or corrective action by the Council.

(g) Applicants who report insufficient CDE credits for the one year period will be ineligible for licence renewal.

*Any*

(5) applicant who cannot meet the CDE requirements because of illness, emergency, or hardship may apply to the Council in writing for a waiver. Waiver requests will be on a case-by-case basis.

*Any the*

(6) licence holder suspended for disciplinary reasons is not exempt from CDE requirements as listed in section (1).

(7) Council Approved Program sponsors for CE credit The Council has approved the following as program sponsors:

(a) An accredited dental, dental hygiene or dental therapist school or program.

(b) The American Dental Association Continuing Education Review Program, the American Dental Association’s speciality associations, and the American Dental Hygienists Association.

(c) The National Dental Association and its speciality societies and the National Dental Hygienists Association.

(d) The American Medical Association.

(e) The Academy of General Dentistry National Sponsor Approval Program

(f) The National Institutes of Health.

(g) The United States Public Health Services.
