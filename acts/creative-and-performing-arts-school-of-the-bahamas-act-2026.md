### CREATIVE AND PERFORMING ARTS SCHOOL OF THE BAHAMAS ACT, 2026

*AND*

AN ACT TO PROVIDE FOR THE ESTABLISHMENT, GOVERNANCE, ADMINISTRATION OF THE CREATIVE AND PERFORMING ARTS SCHOOL OF THE BAHAMAS AND FOR CONNECTED PURPOSES

[Date of Assent - 23 rd March, 2026] Enacted by the Parliament of The Bahamas

## PART I - PRELIMINARY

**1.** Short title.

This Act may be cited as the Creative and Performing Arts School of The Bahamas Act, 2026.

**2.** Interpretation.

*In*

this Act —

“Board” means the Board of Governors established under section 8;

“bye-laws” means the internal rules made by the Board under section 25;

“Minister” means The Minister responsible for Education;

“President” means the President of CAPAS appointed under section 10;

“intellectual property” means any intellectual property rights as defined under the laws of The Bahamas and includes copyright and related rights, trademarks, geographical indications, industrial designs, patents, layout-designs and (topographies) of integrated circuits and protection of undisclosed information;

“Registrar” means the officer appointed under section 12;

“Reserve Fund” means the fund established under section 15.

Objectives.

*The*

objectives of this Act are to —

(a) provide tertiary-level education and technical training in the creative, performing and production arts;

(b) assess and confer degrees, diplomas, certificates and such other awards as approved by the Board;

(c) reserve, develop and promote the cultural identity of The Bahamas; and

(d) facilitate the export and international recognition of Bahamian creative, performing and production arts.

## PART II – ESTABLISHMENT OF THE CREATIVE AND PERFORMING ARTS SCHOOL OF THE BAHAMAS

**4.** Establishment of the Creative and Performing Arts School of The Bahamas.

(1) There is hereby established an educational institution to be known as the Creative and Performing Arts School of The Bahamas (“CAPAS”). (2) Subject to subsection (3), CAPAS shall be a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal and shall, in that name, be capable of —

(a) suing and being sued;

(b) taking, purchasing, or otherwise acquiring charging, holding and disposing of property, moveable or immovable; and

(c) doing or performing all such other things or acts for the performance of its functions under, and for the furtherance, of this Act which may lawfully be done or performed by a body corporate. (3) CAPAS shall not dispose of any land without the prior approval of the House of Assembly by resolution.

**5.** Common seal.

*The*

common seal of CAPAS shall —

(a) be kept in the custody of the Board and shall be affixed to instruments in the presence of the Chairman and Registrar;

(b) be authenticated by the signatures of the Chairman or any other member of the Board authorised by the Board in that behalf, and of the Registrar; and

when affixed to any document and authenticated, be judicially and officially noticed, and, until the contrary is proved, any necessary authorisation of the Board for that document shall be presumed to have been given.

*Functions*

**6.** of CAPAS.

*The*

functions of CAPAS are to —

(a) create, commission, produce, promote and distribute cultural works nationally and internationally, including the production of national events;

(b) undertake research, development and public engagement in the creative, performing and production arts;

(c) foster entrepreneurship, innovation and commercialisation within the creative economy;

(d) establish affiliations and partnerships with institutions and individuals within and outside of The Bahamas;

(e) support and participate in the development of policies and plans that impact the development of the creative economy;

(f) develop and participate in initiatives to expand wider community access, engagement and participation in the creative, performing and production arts;

(g) support the development and implementation of extra-curricular programmes in the creative, performing and production arts in primary, secondary and tertiary education;

(h) initiate, support, develop and implement career pathways for students and alumni of CAPAS;

(i) serve as a repository for creative and cultural work; and (j) provide support and promote the well-being of CAPAS students.

*Affiliated*

**7.** academies.

*CAPAS*

*the*

may, with the approval of the Board, establish affiliated academies, conservatories, or other bodies, whether located in The Bahamas or abroad, for furtherance of the objectives of this Act.

## PART III – GOVERNANCE

*Board of*

Governors.

There is hereby established a Board of Governors.

CAPAS shall be governed by the Board.

The Schedule shall have effect with respect to the constitution and procedures of the Board.

**9.** Powers and duties of the Board.

(1) The Board shall have general direction and control of CAPAS and shall —

(a) approve the strategic plans, budgets and policies of CAPAS;

(b) direct, manage and control the property, revenue and funds of CAPAS;

(c) regulate the employment, conditions of service, and overall discipline of staff;

(d) fix tuition and other fees payable to CAPAS; and

(e) establish committees, including an Advisory Committee in accordance with section 11.

(2) The Board shall not be subject to influence from political, religious, or other external bodies, and shall protect CAPAS from such influence.

**10.** President of CAPAS.

(1) There shall be a President of CAPAS appointed by the Governor-General on the recommendation of the Board.

(2) The President shall serve for a term not exceeding five years and shall be eligible for reappointment.

(3) The Governor-General may, on the recommendation of the Board, remove the President on the grounds of —

(a) misconduct;

(b) physical and mental incapacity; or

(c) persistent failure to perform duties.

(4) The President shall be responsible for the day-to-day management of CAPAS and such other functions as may be conferred by this Act or by the Board.

**11.** Advisory Committee.

(1) There shall be established an Advisory Committee comprised of staff, and student representatives appointed in accordance with bye-laws made by the Board.

The Advisory Committee shall advise the Board on matters relating to —

(a) curriculum and academic standards;

(b) artistic programming and performance quality;

cultural and creative industry practice, standards, networks and opportunities;

(d) student admission, progression, and assessment;

(e) research, creative practice, and innovation.

**12.** Registrar.

(1) The Board shall appoint a Registrar who shall be responsible for —

(a) the administration of admissions;

(b) the maintenance of academic records;

(c) the certification of awards; and

(d) the implementation of academic regulations.

(2) The Registrar shall report directly to the Chairman of the Board or any person the Chairman may designate in writing.

## PART IV – FINANCIAL PROVISIONS

**13.** Funds and resources.

(1) The funds and resources of CAPAS shall consist of —

(a) sums provided by Parliament for the functions of CAPAS;

(b) all fees and charges payable to CAPAS;

(c) gifts, donations, sponsorships, endowments, property and other contributions lawfully received by CAPAS;

(d) income derived from fundraising, productions, services, intellectual property, commercial ventures or other lawful activities; and

(e) any sums or property that become payable or vested in CAPAS in respect of any matter incidental to its functions.

(2) The financial operations of CAPAS shall be governed in accordance with any other enactment for the time being in force relating to public finance management.

*Gifts*

**14.** and grants.

(1) CAPAS shall notify the Minister of any gift, grant or property received from persons including national and international bodies.

(2) The Board shall give effect to any lawful stipulation attached to such gift, grant or property.

Reserve Fund.

CAPAS shall establish a Reserve Fund.

The management of the Reserve Fund, the sums to be carried from time to time to the credit thereof, and the application thereof, shall be determined by the Board, but no part of the Reserve Fund shall be applied otherwise than for the purposes of CAPAS.

(3) At the end of each financial year, any funds standing to the credit of CAPAS and not required for any current purpose shall, after consultation with the Minister of Finance, be carried to the Reserve Fund.

**16.** Borrowing powers.

(1) CAPAS may borrow money, issue bonds or debentures, or otherwise raise capital with the approval of the Minister and the consent of the Minister of Finance.

(2) Any money borrowed under subsection (1) shall be applied only for purposes consistent with the objectives of this Act and functions of CAPAS.

**17.** Accounts and audit.

(1) CAPAS shall keep proper records in relation to the accounts, and shall prepare in respect of each financial year, a statement of accounts. (2) The accounts of CAPAS for each financial year shall be audited annually by auditors appointed by the Board with the approval of the Minister from among members of The Bahamas Institute of Chartered Accountants. (3) CAPAS shall, three months after the end of each financial year, submit a copy of the audited accounts to the Minister, together with a copy of the report made by the auditor.

(4) The Minister shall cause a copy of the annual financial statement of accounts together with the auditor's report to be laid before both chambers of Parliament.

**18.** Budget estimates and annual report.

(1) CAPAS shall prepare for each financial year an annual budget of revenue and expenditure.

(2) The annual budget referred to under subsection (1), shall be submitted to the Minister four months before the beginning of the financial year. CAPAS shall, three months after the end of each financial year, submit to the Minister a report of its activities, accompanied by a statement of its expenditure and revenue.

The Minister shall cause a copy of the report of CAPAS to be laid before both chambers of Parliament.

## PART V – MISCELLANEOUS

**19.** Minister's directions.

(1) Subject to subsection (2), the Minister may, after consultation with the Board, give the Board policy directions of a general nature which the Minister determines to be in the public interest and the Board shall give effect to such directions.

(2) Directions given by the Minister under subsection (1) shall not apply to —

(a) the appointment, termination of appointment, promotion, or discipline of any instructor, administration of other staff of CAPAS;

(b) the admission of any student to CAPAS, evaluation, discipline, academic promotion, certification or issue of awards to students of CAPAS; or

(c) the determination of curriculum, artistic programming, research, or other academic matters.

**20.** Intellectual property.

(1) Intellectual property created by an employee of CAPAS in the course of his or her employment shall vest in CAPAS.

(2) Intellectual property created by a student in the course of study shall vest in the student except where —

(a) the intellectual property is created pursuant to a project funded wholly or substantially by CAPAS;

(b) the intellectual property is created jointly with an employee of CAPAS and is not subject to a written agreement which provides otherwise;

(c) the work is created using substantial institutional resources of CAPAS; or

(d) the student has entered into a written agreement assigning, licensing, or otherwise regulating ownership of the intellectual property.

(3) For the purposes of this section, “substantial institutional resources” does not include—

(a) ordinary classroom instruction;

(b) general access to library, rehearsal, or studio facilities; or standard equipment and services routinely provided to students in the normal course of instruction.

(4) CAPAS may enter into written agreements providing for shared ownership, licensing arrangements, revenue sharing, or other mutually agreed intellectual property arrangements.

**21.** Fees.

CAPAS may impose, publish and collect fees for admission to performances, use of facilities, and such other goods and services provided by CAPAS.

**22.** Exemption from Real Property Tax.

CAPAS shall be exempt from the payment of Real Property Tax in respect of any land and facility owned or occupied by CAPAS.

**23.** Protection of rights.

No person shall be excluded by reason of religious beliefs, political opinions, sexual orientation, disability, age, race, ethnicity, national origin or gender from admission as staff or student of CAPAS or from office or employment therein or to receive any degree or other qualification awarded by CAPAS or from any advantage or privilege thereof.

**24.** Regulations.

The Minister may, after consultation with the Board, make regulations for the better administration of this Act.

**25.** Bye-laws.

The Board may make bye-laws, in accordance with this Act and any regulations made under this Act, for the governance of CAPAS, including —

(a) the constitution and procedures of the Advisory Committee;

(b) codes of conduct and disciplinary procedures for staff and students;

(c) academic and artistic regulations, including admission, assessment, progression and awards;

(d) procedures for recognising the student government association;

(e) the management of intellectual property and revenue-sharing arrangements;

(f) procedures governing commercial activities, fundraising, donations, endowments and student support funds.

### CONSTITUTION AND PROCEDURES OF THE BOARD

**1.** Constitution.

(1) Subject to subparagraph (2), the Board shall consist of a minimum of seven and maximum of nine members appointed by the Minister of whom —

(a) one shall be the President, ex officio;

(b) one shall be a counsel and attorney of at least ten years standing at The Bahamas Bar nominated by the Minister;

(c) one shall be a person with relevant qualification or experience in finance;

(d) one shall be a person with relevant qualification and experience in human resources management;

(e) one shall be a person with relevant qualification and experience in fundraising, communications and marketing;

(f) one shall be a person with relevant qualification and experience in digital technology;

(g) one shall be a representative of the arts and cultural sector;

(h) one shall be a representative of the academic sector;

(i) one shall be a person nominated by the Minister;

(j) one shall be a representative from the private sector with senior executive-level experience in enterprise leadership, creative industries development, capital investment, infrastructure development, tourism and hospitality, or commercial institutional management, and who are not otherwise appointed under paragraphs (b) to (i);

(2) Notwithstanding subparagraph (1), no more than three members of the Board shall be public officers.

(3) At the first constitution of the Board, the Minister shall appoint one-third of the members to hold office for one year, one-third for two years, and the remainder for three years; thereafter all Board appointments shall be for a period of three years to ensure appointments are made on a staggered basis.

The Governor-General shall appoint a Chairman from among the members of the Board.

The Minister shall appoint the Deputy Chairman from among the members of the Board.

(6) The Deputy Chairman shall act as Chairman whenever the Chairman is absent, unable to act, or the office of Chairman is vacant.

**2.** Tenure of office.

Members of the Board shall hold office for a period not exceeding three years and shall be eligible for reappointment.

**3.** Vacancies.

*If*

*of*

*of*

any vacancy occurs in the membership of the Board, such vacancy, shall be filled by the appointment of another member who shall, subject to the provisions this Schedule, hold office for the remainder of the period for which the previous member was appointed, and such appointment shall be made in the same manner and from the same category of persons, if any, as the appointment the previous member.

**4.** Resignation.

(1) The Chairman, may at any time resign his or her office by instrument in writing addressed to the Governor-General and transmitted through the Minister, and the resignation shall take effect from the date of receipt by the Minister of the instrument.

(2) A member of the Board, other than the Chairman may at any time resign his or her office by instrument in writing addressed to the Minister, and the resignation shall take effect from the date of receipt by the Minister of the instrument.

**5.** Revocation.

The Minister may, by instrument, revoke the appointment of a member of the Board upon being satisfied that the member —

(a) is an undischarged bankrupt;

(b) is incapacitated by physical or mental illness;

(c) has been absent, without leave of the Board, from three or more consecutive meetings of the Board;

(d) has been convicted of an indictable offence; or

(e) is otherwise unable or unfit to discharge the functions of a member of the Board.

Publication.

The names of all members of the Board, as first constituted and every change in membership, shall be published in the Gazette.

Meetings.

The Board shall meet at least once every quarter and at such times as may be necessary for the transaction of its business, and such meetings shall be held at such places and times as the Board may determine.

(2) The Chairman may at any time call a special meeting of the Board within seven days of the receipt of a written request for that purpose addressed to the Chairman, by any two members of the Board.

(3) The Chairman shall preside at all meetings of the Board, and the Deputy Chairman shall preside in the Chairman's absence.

(4) If the Chairman and the Deputy Chairman are absent from a meeting, the members present and constituting a quorum shall elect one of their number to preside at that meeting.

(5) The quorum of the Board shall consist of one-half plus one of its members.

(6) The decision of the Board shall be by a majority of votes, and in addition to an original vote, the Chairman or other person presiding at a meeting shall have a casting vote in any case in which the voting is equal. (7) The Board shall cause minutes of every meeting to be recorded in proper form and the minutes of each meeting shall be confirmed by the Board at the next meeting.

(8) The validity of the proceedings of the Board shall not be affected by any vacancy.

(9) Subject to this Act, the Board may regulate its own proceedings. (10) The Board may invite a person who in the opinion of the Board has expert knowledge concerning any functions of the Board which is likely to be of assistance to attend and participate in any meeting of the Board but the person is not qualified to vote.

**9.** Committees.

(1) The Board may appoint such committees for the proper carrying out of the functions of the Board, as it considers necessary and may delegate to any committee such functions as the Board may determine.

(2) Each of the standing committees shall have such functions as prescribed.

**10.** Remuneration.

There shall be paid from the funds of the Board to the Chairman and other members of the Board such remuneration whether by way of honorarium or allowances, as the Minister may determine.

Disclosure of interest.

A member of the Board or a committee established by the Board who has a direct or indirect pecuniary interest, or other interest, in a matter under consideration by the Board or committee shall disclose the nature of that member's interest at the next meeting of the Board or committee and that member shall not participate in the consideration of, or vote on, any question relating to that matter.

**12.** Confidentiality.

A member of the Board or a committee established by the Board, the Registrar or any person acting on their behalf shall preserve confidentiality with regard to the information obtained in the course of their duties and employment under this Act, and shall not disclose such information to any other person except —

(a) as may be required in the administration of this Act;

(b) to their counsel and attorney; or

(c) with the consent of the person to whom the information relates and in accordance with any other enactment for the time being in force relating to data protection.

**13.** Protection of members.

No action, prosecution or other proceedings shall be brought or instituted personally against any member of the Board in respect of any act done in good faith in pursuance or execution or intended execution of this Act.
