c No. 67 of 2025 NATIONAL HEALTH INSURANCE ACT, 2025

AN ACT TO CONTINUE THE NATIONAL HEALTH INSURANCE PLAN AND FUND ESTABLISHED AND EXISTING UNDER THE NATIONAL HEALTH INSURANCE ACT 2016; TO EXTEND THE BENEFITS UNDER THE PLAN; AND TO PROVIDE AS A MINIMUM, STANDARD HEALTH BENEFITS AND OTHER BENEFITS TO PERSONS PRESENTLY ENROLLED IN THE PLAN AND TO PERSONS ELIGIBLE AND ENROLLED AFTER THE COMMENCEMENT OF THIS ACT; TO VALIDATE ALL ACTS DONE UNDER THE EXISTING PLAN AND FUND; TO REPEAL AND REPLACE THE NATIONAL HEALTH INSURANCE ACT 2016 AND FOR CONNECTED PURPOSES

[Date of Assent 28th August 2025] Enacted by the Parliament of The Bahamas Enacted by the Parliament of The Bahamas

**1.** Short title and commencement.

(1) This Act may be cited as the National Health Insurance Act, 2025. (2) This Act shall come into operation on a date to be appointed by the Minister by notice published in the Gazette and different dates may be appointed by the Minister for the coming into operation of different provisions of this Act.

**2.** Interpretation.

(1) In this Act —

“actuary” means a person who analyses the financial cost of risk and uncertainty and is a member in good standing of a professional body of internationally recognized actuaries;

“approved insurer” means an insurer registered to carry on sickness or health insurance business by the Insurance Commission of The Bahamas, and is approved by the Authority to enter an agreement under section 34 to participate in the Plan;

“Authority” means the National Health Insurance Authority established under the repealed Act and continued under section 5(1);

“benefits” means the standard health benefits or other health benefits provided to a beneficiary under the Plan or provided to an insured person by an approved insurer;

“beneficiary” means a person enrolled under the Plan to receive benefits;

“Board” means the governing body of the Authority as constituted under section 5(2);

“contract of insurance” means a contract between an approved insurer and an insured person to provide sickness and health coverage to the insured person;

“enrolled” is the status of a person registered under the Plan and entitled to benefits;

“health care services” means the services provided in the standard health benefits specified in the Third Schedule;

“health professionals” means persons registered and licensed under the Health Professions Act, (Ch. 233) to provide health care services;

“Insurance Commission” means the Insurance Commission of The Bahamas established under section 4 of the Insurance Act (Ch. 347);

“insured person” means a person for whom standard health benefits are provided by an approved insurer under the terms and conditions of a contract of insurance;

“Minister” means the Minister charged with responsibility for National Health Insurance;

“National Health Insurance Fund” or “the Fund” means the National Health Insurance Fund continued under section 25;

“nurse” means a registered nurse under the Nurses and Midwives Act, 2023 (No. 9 of 2023):

“other health benefits” means the health benefits outside the scope of standard health benefits as prescribed by regulations;

“Plan” means the National Health Insurance Plan established under the repealed Act and continued under section 27;

“primary health care services” means the first level of health care that focuses on prevention, and addresses and coordinates all essential health needs including referrals to a specialist medical practitioner;

“Public Finance Management Act” means the Public Finance Management Act 2023 (No. 4 of 2023);

“repealed Act” means the National Health Insurance Act, 2016 (No. 29 of 2016);

“standard health benefits” or “benefits” means the services specified in the Third Schedule;

“Standard Health Benefits Network” or “Network” means the group of standard health benefits providers contracted by the Authority to deliver the scope of standard health benefits and other health benefits;

“standard health benefits provider” or “provider” means a medical practitioner, a nurse, or a health professional who provides standard health benefits and other health benefits to a beneficiary and an insured person as a part of the group comprising the Standard Health Benefits Network.

(2) For the purposes of this Act, a person is ordinarily resident in The Bahamas if The Bahamas is home for that person for not less than six months before the application for registration.

(3) A person’s ordinary residence under subsection (2), shall not be deemed to have been interrupted by that person’s occasional or temporary absence from The Bahamas or that person’s absence from The Bahamas because of —

(a) occupation or employment, or that of his spouse or, if a minor, his parent or legal guardian, in the public service or in the foreign service of The Bahamas serving in a mission abroad or a principal representative of The Bahamas in another country or accredited to an international organization, and that occupation or employment requires him, his spouse or, if a minor, his parent or legal guardian to travel outside of The Bahamas; or

(b) absence in pursuit of a course of higher education, or such pursuit by his spouse, or, if a minor, his parent, or legal guardian, and such a programme involves occasional or regular residence outside The Bahamas.

**3.** Objectives of the Act.

*The*

objectives of the Act are to —

(a) establish the administrative framework for accessible, affordable, and quality standard health benefits or other health benefits, for beneficiaries;

(b) establish the standard health benefits as the minimum standard of coverage across all sickness and health insurance products; provide equal opportunity for public and private sector participation in the provision of standard health benefits or other health benefits; enable the sustainability of standard health benefits or other health benefits through appropriate allocation of resources;

promote efficiency in providing standard health benefits or other health benefits;

(f) improve the overall health of the population of The Bahamas by the attainment of universal health coverage.

**4.** Public health emergency powers.

(1) Where an order is made by the Minister responsible for health declaring a part of The Bahamas to be an area infected by an infectious disease specified in section 29 (2) (a) (ix) of the Health Services Act (Ch. 231), the Minister, after consultation with the Authority, may make orders necessary to address the infectious disease as a public health emergency. (2) An order made by the Minister under subsection (1) may include the following temporary measures —

(a) authorizing the Standard Health Benefits Provider Network to deliver health care services during the infectious disease public health emergency; or

(b) amending the Third Schedule.

(3) An order made under subsection (2) shall state the duration of the order and shall provide extensions to the order, where the Minister responsible for health considers it necessary in the public interest.

### II - NATIONAL HEALTH INSURANCE AUTHORITY FUNCTIONS AND POWERS

**5.** Continuation of the National Health Insurance Authority.

(1) The body corporate established under the repealed Act as the National Health Insurance Authority is continued.

(2) The governing body of the Authority shall be a Board that performs the functions and exercises the powers of the Authority under this Act. (3) The provisions of the First Schedule shall have effect concerning the constitution and procedure of the Board and otherwise.

*Seal*

**6.** of Authority.

*The*

seal of the Authority shall be —

(a) kept in the custody of the Chairman, Deputy Chairman, or another person, as the Board may direct;

(b) affixed by the Secretary to the Board in the presence of a member of the Board designated by the Chairman, and one other member of the Board;

authenticated by the signature of the Chairman or Deputy Chairman and the Secretary to the Board.

*Functions*

of the Authority.

*The*

functions of the Authority are to —

(a) administer the Plan and provide the standard health benefits and other health benefits to beneficiaries;

(b) conduct periodic reviews of the Plan;

(c) modify the standard health benefits and other health benefits under the Plan, in consultation with the Minister and the Minister of Finance;

(d) establish and implement mechanisms for —

(i) quality assurance of the standard health benefits and other health benefits delivered under the Plan;

(ii) promotion and delivery of wellness services and initiatives in providing standard health benefits and other health benefits; (e) establish the criteria for registration of a standard health benefits provider and other health benefits providers in the Plan;

(f) enrol persons eligible to receive benefits under the Plan under section 28;

(g) maintain a register of standard health benefits providers and other health benefits providers participating in the Plan and publish, from time to time, a list of standard health benefits and other health benefits providers;

(h) ensure appropriate coverage by providers of the standard health benefits through the Standard Health Benefits Network or by providers of other health benefits to beneficiaries in all islands of The Bahamas;

(i) oversee the Standard Health Benefits Network and ensure compliance with the terms of the agreement between the Authority and providers;

(j) manage and control the Fund, and keep the Fund under constant review;

(k) produce an annual report on the work of the Authority and the Authority’s performance under this Act;

(l) cause a review of the Act every two years after the review of the Act under section 60, and submit a report of the review to the Minister;

(m) investigate a breach of a provision of this Act and refer the results of the investigation to the regulator of the provider or approved insurer;

(n) partner with governmental, non-governmental, regional or international agencies to undertake short-term and long-term initiatives that provide health benefits outside the scope of the standard health benefits; and

(o) do or cause to be done, other things as may be conferred by this Act, or other written law, or that are necessary to enable the Authority to carry out its functions

*Powers of*

**8.** Authority.

*The*

(1) Authority shall —

(a) enter into agreements for the provision of standard health benefits with approved insurers;

(b) establish a fee schedule for the standard health benefits and other health benefits outlined in the Third Schedule, utilizing a fee-setting process with providers, approved insurers, and other relevant bodies;

(c) regulate the maximum premiums paid by an insured person to approved insurers for standard health benefits;

(d) deliver standard health benefits and other health benefits under the Plan;

(e) invest available funds for the benefit of the Fund;

(f) appoint officers, employees, and agents as the Authority considers necessary;

(g) enter into written agreements with —

(i) entities for the administration of the Plan, to manage and administer the standard health benefits and other health benefits under the Plan;

(ii) providers for the provision of standard health benefits under the Plan;

(iii) providers for the provision of other health benefits under the Plan;

(h) conduct investigations and regular audits, and cause on-site inspections of providers under section 11;

(i) require a person to produce information under section 9; (j) appoint an actuary to conduct, from time to time, actuarial projections and reviews, and to calculate estimates of per capita costs of the Plan and to produce a report for the Minister; (k) appoint committees of the Board comprising two members of the Board and other persons as the Board thinks fit, to provide operational support to the Board, and a committee so appointed shall regulate its procedure;

delegate a power or function upon terms and conditions to be agreed, except that where the exercise of the delegation of the power or function results in expenditure which is likely to exceed one per cent of the annual budget of the Fund, the delegation shall be subject to the approval of the Minister;

(m) do or cause to be done, the things authorized by this Act or other written law or do or cause to be done other things deemed necessary to enable the Authority to carry out its powers. (2) Subject to the provisions of this Act, a power or function which the Authority may exercise or perform under this Act may be delegated to a member of the Board, committee appointed under this Act, or an employee, subject to the terms, conditions or restrictions as the Board may determine and the Board shall not be divested of the power or function delegated.

**9.** Power of the Authority to require information.

(1) The Authority may require a —

(a) provider or approved insurer, to supply information or to produce for examination, records kept under section 42;

(b) beneficiary to supply information as the Authority may reasonably require for enabling the Authority to perform its functions under this Act;

(c) body specified in the Second Schedule to supply information as the Authority may reasonably require for the performance of its functions under this Act.

(2) A provider or approved insurer who fails to produce a record or to supply information as required by subsection (1), commits an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding ten thousand dollars.

### MANAGEMENT OF AUTHORITY

**10.** Appointment of Managing Director.

(1) The Board shall appoint a Managing Director as the chief executive officer of the Board, who shall —

(a) have responsibility for the day-to-day operations of the Authority; and

(b) perform the duties assigned by the Board.

The Managing Director shall hold office on the terms and conditions as the Board may determine.

The Board may appoint other officers, employees, and agents at the remuneration and on the terms and conditions it considers necessary or appropriate for the proper conduct of business.

**11.** Appointment of inspectors and powers of inspectors.

(1) The Authority may appoint a person to conduct on-site inspections for quality assurance and patient safety on the premises of providers. (2) A person appointed under subsection (1) shall, when so directed by the Authority —

(a) enter the premises of a provider, at reasonable times and after notice to the provider;

(b) inspect and examine the premises, equipment, procedures, and information technology systems of the provider;

(c) make enquiries necessary for ascertaining whether the provisions of this Act are being or have been complied with;

(d) interview persons on the premises whom the person inspecting has reasonable cause to believe, is the provider;

(e) examine the claims, data, and accounting records in the possession of the provider; and

(f) exercise the powers of inspection necessary for the purposes of the proper administration of this Act.

(3) A person appointed by the Authority under subsection (1) shall be furnished with a certificate of appointment and on request for admission to premises, shall produce the certificate of appointment, which shall be sufficient proof of his authority to inspect the premises.

**12.** Inspection report.

(1) A person appointed under section 11 and directed by the Authority to inspect the premises of a provider shall submit a report to the Authority after every inspection.

(2) The report submitted under subsection (1) shall contain –

(a) information on the findings of the person inspecting the premises;

(b) information which may indicate an alleged violation of this Act; and

(c) recommendations for further action.

(3) On receipt of a report containing information specified in subsection (2) (b), the Authority shall forward the report to the Compliance Officer for an investigation.

The Authority shall forward the report received under subsection (2) to the provider.

Appointment and functions of Compliance Officer.

The Board shall, after consultation with the Managing Director, appoint a compliance officer (“the Compliance Officer”).

(2) The Compliance Officer shall have power to –

(a) educate beneficiaries, providers, and approved insurers of their rights and responsibilities under this Act.

(b) investigate non-compliance with the provisions of this Act by approved insurers, and by beneficiaries; and

(c) investigate alleged violations of this Act by a provider referred to the Compliance Officer under section 12 (3).

(3) The Compliance Officer shall, on completion of an investigation under this section, forward the results of the investigation to the Authority together with recommendations which may include –

(a) a referral to the regulator or other body having authority over a provider;

(b) a referral to the Insurance Commission of the results of the investigation of an approved insurer under subsection 2 (b);

(c) a referral to the Commissioner of Police of the results of an investigation of a beneficiary under subsection 2 (b) or a provider under subsection 2 (c) if the investigation indicates the commission of an offence; or

(d) the termination of the contract with a provider or approved insurer.

### FINANCIAL PROVISIONS

**14.** Application of the Public Finance Management Act.

*The*

following requirements of the Public Finance Management Act (No.4 of 2023) apply to the Authority —

(a) accounting standards for the preparation of financial statements as to the revenue, expenditure, assets, and liabilities of the Authority;

(b) incorporation of annual plans in the annual budget of the Authority; and

(c) reporting requirements.

**15.** Funds and resources of the Authority.

(1) The funds and resources of the Authority shall consist of —

(a) monies provided by Parliament;

(b) monies accrued to the Authority from its operations;

(c) monies borrowed by the Authority under section 17;

donations made to the Authority;

monies payable by an employer of an insured person, or monies or allowances payable by a self-employed person for standard health benefits or other health benefits under this Act;

(f) contributions by beneficiaries as may be prescribed (g) monies paid to, or property vested in, the Authority; and (h) all other monies lawfully received by the Authority.

(2) There shall be paid, from the funds of the Authority —

(a) salaries, allowances, loans, pensions and gratuities of the staff of the Authority;

(b) such remuneration, reasonable travelling and subsistence allowances as the Board may determine for members of the Board or officers of the Authority, when engaged in the business of the Authority; and

(c) other expenses incurred by the Authority in the performance of its functions.

**16.** Accounts and records.

(1) The Authority shall cause to be kept proper books of account and other records relating to its accounts.

(2) The accounts of the Authority shall be audited annually by an independent auditor licensed by, and in good standing with, The Bahamas Institute of Chartered Accountants.

(3) The independent auditor shall be appointed by the Authority and the fees of the independent auditor shall be paid by the Authority.

(4) The Authority shall submit a report of its activities for a financial year to the Minister, not later than one hundred and twenty days after the end of the financial year.

(5) The report specified in subsection (4), shall include —

(a) information on the financial affairs of the Authority;

(b) an audited balance sheet;

(c) an audited statement of income and expenditure; and

(d) other information as the Minister may require.

(6) Three months after the end of each financial year, the Authority shall submit a copy of the audited accounts to the Minister, together with a copy of the report of the auditor.

(7) Notwithstanding subsection (5), the Authority shall, upon the written request of the Minister, furnish the Minister with information of the property and activity of the Authority, to enable the Minister to verify the information furnished.

The Minister shall lay a copy of the audited accounts before each House of Parliament, together with a copy of the report made by the auditor on the accounts.

**17.** Borrowing powers.

(1) Subject to subsection (2) and section 59 of the Public Debt Management Act, 2021 (No. 6 of 2021), the Authority may borrow sums required by it for meeting its obligations or discharging its functions, and may in respect of the borrowing, issue debentures or other securities in the form as the Authority may determine.

(2) Borrowing by the Authority under subsection (1) shall be approved by the Minister of Finance after consultation with the Minister.

**18.** Advances and guarantees of borrowings.

(1) Subject to subsection (2), the Minister of Finance may, with the approval of the Minister —

(a) make advances to the Authority for the purpose of enabling the Authority to defray expenditure and of providing working capital for the Authority;

(b) provide guarantees for the repayment of the principal, and the payment of interest and other charges on borrowings of the Authority made pursuant to section 17, as the Minister thinks fit. (2) No advances shall be made to the Authority, and no guarantee shall be given by the Minister of Finance under this section, unless prior approval has been granted by affirmative resolution of each House of Parliament. (3) Where a sum is paid pursuant to a guarantee under this section, the Minister of Finance shall, after the end of each financial year, beginning in the year in which the sum is issued and ending with the year in which all liability in respect of the principal and interest is discharged, lay before the House of Assembly a statement relating to that sum.

(4) A sum required by the Minister of Finance for making an advance and discharging a guarantee under this section shall be charged on, and issued out of, the Consolidated Fund.

**19.** Payment of, and interest on, advances and sums issued to meet guarantee.

(1) The Authority shall make to the Minister of Finance at the times and in the manner as the Minister of Finance may direct, payment of —

(a) sums in repayment of an advance made to the Authority;

(b) sums issued in fulfilment of a guarantee by the Minister of Finance under section 18; and

interest on the amount outstanding on the sums advanced at rates as the Minister of Finance may direct.

The Minister of Finance shall lay before the House of Assembly a statement of payments due from the Authority under subsection (1).

**20.** Reserve fund.

(1) Monies of the National Health Insurance Fund which are not immediately required to be expended to meet the obligations of the Authority or to discharge its functions under this Act, shall be paid into a reserve fund. (2) The Authority shall determine the —

(a) sums to be credited to a reserve fund;

(b) management of a reserve fund; and

(c) use of a reserve fund.

(3) No part of a reserve fund shall be applied otherwise than for the objectives of this Act.

(4) The Minister may make regulations governing a reserve fund.

**21.** Power to invest.

(1) The Authority may, subject to the approval of the Minister of Finance, and the provisions of the Public Debt Management Act, 2021 (No. 6 of 2021) invest monies in —

(a) securities issued or guaranteed by the Government; and

(b) securities, real estate, financial contracts, agreements, and investments, authorized by the Minister on the recommendation of the Authority.

(2) The Authority shall not invest in securities or property under subsection (1)(b) which are outside The Bahamas without the general or special directions of the Minister, the approval of the Minister of Finance and other regulatory approvals required under a written law.

*The in*

## PART III - NATIONAL HEALTH INSURANCE FUND

**25.** Continuation of National Health Insurance Fund.

(1) The National Health Insurance Fund established under the repealed Act is continued for the purpose of financing the cost of standard health benefits, or other health benefits under the Plan.

(2) The Fund shall be under the control and management of the Authority.

## PART IV - NATIONAL HEALTH INSURANCE PLAN

**26.** Validation of National Health Insurance Plan.

The establishment of the National Health Insurance Plan and all acts done in implementing the Plan under the provisions of Parts III, IV, and V of the repealed Act, are declared valid, lawful, and effectual as if the provisions had been brought into effect on 5 April 2017, and continued until the commencement of this Act.

**27.** Continuation of National Health Insurance Plan.

The National Health Insurance Plan validated under section 26 is continued under this Act, and persons enrolled under the repealed Act shall become beneficiaries and are entitled to receive benefits under this Act.

### ENROLMENT IN PLAN

**28.** Eligibility for enrolment in the Plan.

(1) A person is eligible to enrol in the Plan if he —

(a) is ordinarily resident in The Bahamas, being either —

(i) a citizen of The Bahamas; or

(ii) a lawful resident of The Bahamas under the Immigration Act (Ch. 191); or

(b) is enrolled under the repealed Act; and

is registered with the National Insurance Board and possesses a National Insurance card.

Where a person enrolled under subsection (1) (b) has a contract of insurance, at the commencement of this Act, the costs of the benefits to the person covered under the contract of insurance shall be paid by the approved insurer.

(3) A person may be enrolled in the Plan, notwithstanding that the person has benefits under a contract of insurance by an approved insurer commensurate with the benefits covered under the standard health benefits.

(4) The cost of the standard health benefits of a person who has benefits under a contract of insurance with an approved insurer shall be paid by the approved insurer.

(5) Where a person enrolled in the Plan under subsection (1) (b) or subsection

(3) loses his private sickness and health insurance due to the loss of employment or otherwise, the Authority shall provide benefits to that person unless that person notifies the Authority in writing of his desire not to continue to be enrolled in the Plan.

**29.** Ineligibility to enrol in the Plan.

*A*

person is not eligible to enrol in the Plan if he —

(a) fails to meet the criteria of eligibility in section 28;

(b) is employed by an international organization declared by Order under section 3 of the International Organizations (Immunities and Privileges) Act (Ch.14) to be an organization of which the Government of The Bahamas and one or more foreign sovereign Powers or the government or governments thereof are members.

**30.** Application for enrolment in the Plan.

(1) A person eligible to enrol in the Plan under section 28 may apply to the Authority in the prescribed manner to be enrolled in the Plan.

(2) Where the person eligible to enrol in the Plan under subsection (1) is a minor or is an adult who is unable to make application to be enrolled, the application may be made by a parent or legal guardian.

(3) The Authority may approve the application made under subsection (1) or

(2) and shall notify the person of its decision within ten days of receiving the application.

### BENEFITS AND BENEFICIARIES

Selection of standard health benefit provider.

(1) A person enrolled in the Plan shall be a beneficiary, entitled to the standard health benefits or other health benefits specified in the Third Schedule, and shall select a standard health benefit provider from the list of standard health benefit providers in the Network published by the Authority.

(2) Where a person enrolled in the Plan is a minor or an adult unable to select a provider, the selection may be made by a parent or legal guardian from the list of providers in the Network.

(3) A beneficiary may, once a year, and in the manner prescribed by the Authority, request a change of his provider.

(4) Subsection (3) shall not apply where the beneficiary requesting a change of his provider —

(a) has relocated to another settlement or island, which makes it no longer feasible for the beneficiary to access his provider; or

(b) is not satisfied with the services of his provider.

(5) After the expansion of the standard health benefits under the Plan, a beneficiary who requires the services of specialist medical practitioners may select an additional provider.

**32.** Obligations of beneficiaries.

(1) Every beneficiary shall notify the Authority in the form prescribed, within thirty days of a change of —

(a) his citizenship or residency status; or

(b) other circumstances which impact his eligibility to remain enrolled and to receive benefits under the Plan.

(2) A beneficiary who fails to comply with subsection (1), and continues to receive benefits under the Plan, commits an offence.

**33.** Suspension, termination, etc. of beneficiary status.

(1) The Authority may suspend standard health benefits or other health benefits under the Plan where the Authority finds that the beneficiary —

(a) has failed to select a provider under section 31 (1); or

(b) has not used the services of the provider for a period exceeding two years.

(2) The Authority may suspend or terminate a person from the Plan where —

(a) there is a change in the person’s citizenship or residency status and that person is no longer eligible to be enrolled in the Plan under section 28; or

there is a written request from that person to be removed from the Plan;

the person has died; or

(d) the person is convicted of an offence involving fraud or dishonesty under this Act.

(3) Where benefits are suspended under subsection (1) or suspended or terminated under subsection (2) (a) or (d), the beneficiary may appeal the decision of the Authority to the tribunal established under section 51.

### AGREEMENT WITH APPROVED INSURERS

**34.** Agreement with approved insurers.

(1) An approved insurer shall agree with the Authority to participate in the Plan to provide standard health benefits to an insured person enrolled in the Plan, on the terms and conditions specified in the agreement. (2) Subsection (1) does not apply to an insured person who is covered under a separate insurance contract with another approved insurer and confirmed in writing by one of the insurers.

(3) The Authority shall, before agreeing with an approved insurer, be satisfied with the—

(a) financial stability of the approved insurer;

(b) approved insurer’s compliance with the Insurance Act (Ch. 347); and

(c) ability of the approved insurer to provide standard health benefits, and other health benefits under the Plan.

(4) An agreement referred to in subsection (1), shall contain —

(a) provisions for the —

(i) standard health benefits;

(ii) payment rates to be charged by the provider for standard health benefits;

(iii) method of setting payment rates for standard health benefits;

(iv) terms of the payments to be made by the approved insurer to the provider for standard health benefits;

(v) co-payments or deductibles to be paid by an insured person for standard health benefits;

(vi) procedure for access to standard health benefits by an insured person;

(vii) procedure for processing and managing claims for standard health benefits;

(viii) conduct of financial and clinical audit functions;

enrolment and coverage of an insured person under the Plan who lose private sickness and health insurance coverage;

(x) protection from discrimination against an insured person based on age, gender, or pre-existing conditions;

(xi) responsibilities of the parties under the agreement; and

(xii) other applicable provisions contained in a standard contract of insurance;

(b) information on the process of selecting a provider from the Standard Health Benefits Network;

(c) provisions for access by the Authority to information disclosed under the Data Protection (Privacy of Personal Information) Act (Ch. 324A), not limited to the name, date of birth, and national insurance number of an insured person enrolled under the Plan; and (d) other matters deemed necessary by the Authority and the approved insurer for carrying out the objectives of this Act.

*Duties of*

**35.** approved insurers.

*An*

(1) approved insurer shall —

(a) provide the standard health benefits to every insured person enrolled in the Plan and covered by an agreement with the Authority;

(b) advise the Authority of an insured person who is enrolled in the Plan and whose private sickness and health insurance is lapsed cancelled;

(c) encourage an insured person referred to in paragraph (b) who is eligible to be enrolled in the Plan to apply to be enrolled in the Plan under section 30;

(d) provide proof to the Authority of the issue of a contract of insurance to an insured person also enrolled in the Plan within two weeks of the issue of the contract of insurance;

(e) provide an insured person at the time of execution of a contract of insurance and every year thereafter, with a list of providers under the Standard Health Benefit Network for the purpose of – (i) selecting a provider; or

(ii) opting out of selecting a provider, where the approved insurer notifies an insured person of the impact, financial or otherwise, of making the choice to opt out of selecting a provider;

(f) at the request of the Authority, submit to the Authority information about the contract of insurance of every insured person enrolled in the Plan; and

(g) do, or cause to be done, other things as are necessary, or expedient for providing standard health benefits as an approved insurer. Subject to subsection (3), during the period between the date on which this Act is brought into operation and the date when the standard health benefit is implemented by order of the Minister, no approved insurer shall cancel, refuse to renew, or increase the premiums of an existing sickness and health insurance policy, because the standard health benefit is introduced or added to the policy.

(3) Nothing in subsection (2) shall prohibit an approved insurer from —

(a) making standard premium adjustments, including adjustments at annual renewals; or

(b) denying a person's sickness and health insurance coverage where there is non-disclosure, misrepresentation, or fraud.

**36.** Authority to recommend action against an approved insurer.

(1) Where an approved insurer —

(a) fails to disclose information requested by the Authority about a contract of insurance;

(b) fails to provide appropriate standard health benefits to an insured person, without reasonable cause;

(c) commits, or is found guilty of, an act or omission which makes the approved insurer liable to revocation or suspension of the approved insurer's licence under the Insurance Act (Ch. 347); or

(d) breaches a term of an agreement between the approved insurer and the Authority,

(e) contravenes a provision of this Act, the Authority shall refer these matters to the Insurance Commission to be dealt with under Part III of the Insurance Act (Ch. 347).

(2) The Insurance Commission shall not require a referral from the Authority to enforce a breach by an approved insurer of the provisions of the Insurance Act (Ch. 347).

**37.** Liability of approved insurers.

*An*

approved insurer shall not be relieved of liability under a contract of insurance for the provision of standard health benefits where —

(a) the registration of the approved insurer is cancelled or suspended under the Insurance Act (Ch. 347);

(b) the approved insurer withdraws from the business of providing sickness and health insurance under the Insurance Act (Ch. 347); or

(c) the activities of the approved insurer are restricted under the Insurance Act (Ch. 347).

### STANDARD HEALTH BENEFITS AND OTHER HEALTH BENEFIT PROVIDERS

**38.** Eligibility for standard health benefits providers.

*Upon meets*

application in writing and payment of the prescribed fees, a person who —

(a) the requirements of a law governing the practice —

(i) of medicine and is registered and licensed as a medical practitioner or specialist under the Medical Act (Ch. 224);

(ii) of nursing and is registered and licensed as a nurse under the Nurses and Midwives Act (No 9 of 2023); or

(iii) of a health profession and is registered and licensed under the Health Professions Act (Ch. 233); and

*may*

(b) other requirements as may be prescribed, be approved by the Authority to participate as a standard health benefits provider in the Standard Health Benefits Network.

**39.** Agreement with providers.

(1) A provider approved under section 38 shall agree with the Authority to provide standard health benefits and other health benefits under the Plan to beneficiaries and to every insured person covered under an agreement between the Authority and an approved insurer.

(2) The agreement between the provider and the Authority shall establish —

(a) standards and conditions for the participation of medical practitioners, specialists, nurses and health professionals in the Standard Health Benefits Network;

(b) a procedure by which a beneficiary and an insured person may access health care services, including the referral process;

(c) standard health benefits or other health benefits to be provided under the Plan;

(d) fee schedules, payment rates, and terms of payment for provision of standard health benefits or other health benefits under the Plan;

(e) health care quality assurance and standards of care;

(f) a procedure for the submission of claims for standard health benefits or other health benefits by providers;

(g) a procedure for conduct of on-site clinical inspection for quality assurance and patient safety;

(h) a procedure for the conduct of financial and clinical audits;

(h) access to standard health benefits or other health benefits by a beneficiary and an insured person;

reporting requirements for providers;

(j) a procedure for maintaining electronic health records;

(k) standard claims submission processes by providers;

general responsibilities of the Authority and providers; and (m) the process for necessary data sharing under the Data Protection Act (Privacy of Personal Information) Act (Ch. 324A).

(3) The Authority shall keep a register of all providers and health professionals who have executed agreements with the Authority under subsection (1).

**40.** Functions of providers.

*The*

functions of a provider are to —

(a) provide standard health benefits and other health benefits under the Plan to a beneficiary and an insured person;

(b) provide to the Authority and an approved insurer such information as may be required by the Authority and approved insurer;

(c) record the health information of a beneficiary and an insured person in an electronic health recording system established by the Authority;

(d) submit reports of service and claims electronically to the Authority and the approved insurer; and

(e) carry out other functions as agreed by the provider and the Authority.

**41.** Quality of care by providers.

(1) The Authority may establish guidelines specifying the quality of health care services required of providers under the standard health benefits and other health benefits.

(2) The guidelines established by the Authority under subsection (1) shall cover —

(a) health care services delivered by the provider to a beneficiary and an insured person;

(b) claims for health care services delivered by the provider to every beneficiary and insured person; and

(c) standards of performance required of providers by the Authority.

**42.** Providers and approved insurers to keep and maintain records. (1) A provider and an approved insurer shall, in a manner determined by the Authority, compile and maintain electronic records relating to —

(a) health care services delivered by the provider to a beneficiary and an insured person;

claims for health care services delivered by the provider to a beneficiary and an insured person;

standards of performance required of providers by the Authority; and

(d) other information as the Authority may require.

(2) Subject to the provisions of the Data Protection (Privacy of Personal Information) Act (Ch. 324A), a provider and an approved insurer shall upon written request, provide to the Authority, electronic or physical access to the health records and enrolment status of a beneficiary and insured person, to investigate a contravention of this Act.

*Duty*

**43.** to submit report.

(1) A provider shall submit a report to the Authority in the manner and form determined by the Authority concerning the delivery of health care services to a beneficiary and an insured person, and the health outcome of the beneficiary and the insured person.

(2) The report under subsection (1) shall contain —

(a) the name of the beneficiary and the insured person;

(b) the national insurance number of the beneficiary and the insured person;

(c) the date of birth, sex, and other necessary demographic information of a beneficiary and an insured person;

(d) the financial data relating to the expenditure on delivery of standard health benefits or other health benefits;

(e) utilization data relating to the delivery of standard health benefits or other health benefits;

(f) the evaluation of the efficiency of pathways and the sufficiency of benefits;

(g) the provider satisfaction survey data of a beneficiary, and an insured person;

(h) specific quality and performance data of the provider; and (i) other relevant data requested by the Authority.

**44.** Termination of providers.

*The*

Authority may, in the interest of public health and safety, terminate a provider from participation in the Standard Health Benefits Network or from providing other health benefits, if the provider —

(a) submits a false or fraudulent claim to the Authority for payment;

(b) commits an act of fraud in providing standard health benefits or other health benefits;

(c) fails to disclose material information requested by the Authority;

fails to utilize electronic health records under the policy of the Authority;

fails to comply with a recommendation of the Authority under section 12(2);

(f) discloses confidential data or fails to protect confidential data; (g) fails to meet quality assurance or minimum standards of care; (h) fails to provide benefits to beneficiaries without good cause; (i) does an act, or omits to do an act, that results in the revocation or suspension of his licence to practice under a written law; (j) fails to satisfy the requirements specified in section 38; and (k) breaches a term of the agreement under section 39.

**45.** Withdrawal of providers.

(1) A provider may voluntarily withdraw participation in the Standard Health Benefits Network or withdraw other health benefits by giving the Authority ninety days' written notice of the intention to withdraw, and the provider shall —

(a) inform a beneficiary and an insured person of the withdrawal of the provider from participation in the Standard Health Benefits Network or the provision of other health benefits;

(b) during the period of notice of withdrawal, cooperate with the Authority and assist with the transition of a beneficiary and an insured person affected by the provider’s withdrawal, to another provider; and

(c) discontinue providing benefits to a beneficiary and an insured person after the expiration of the notice of withdrawal.

(2) Upon the expiration of the ninety-day notice period under subsection (1), the provider who withdraws shall no longer be eligible to provide benefits under the Standard Health Benefits Network or to provide other health benefits, and a beneficiary and an insured person who received health benefits from that provider shall no longer be entitled to receive benefits from that provider under the Plan.

(3) A provider who fails to satisfy the requirements of subsection (1) (a) or (b) or provides benefits in contravention of subsection (1) (c), commits an offence.

### V – ADMINISTRATION AND RELATED MATTERS

Transfer of public officers serving in the Authority under the repealed Act.

(1) At the date of the commencement of this Act, all public officers serving in the Authority under the repealed Act shall be transferred with their consent to the service of the Authority.

(3) Where a public officer refuses to be transferred to the Authority under subsection (1), he shall be transferred, with his consent, to the service of a Government Ministry or Department on the terms and conditions of service attached to his appointment as a public officer.

**47.** Pension of persons transferred to the Authority.

(1) A public officer who ceases to be in the service of the Government because of a transfer to the service of the Authority, and who subsequently retires from the service of the Authority in circumstances that, had the public officer remained a public officer he would have been eligible for pension under the Pensions Act (Ch. 43), shall be entitled to a pension from the Authority under subsections (2) and (3).

(2) A pension payable by the Authority to a person referred to in subsection (1), shall be calculated under the Pensions Act (Ch. 43) and paid in respect of his public service and his service with the Authority.

(3) The Minister of Finance may, by warrant, pay out of the Consolidated Fund, contributions to the Authority towards the grant of pensions under this section as may be prescribed.

**48.** Power of the Authority to establish a contributory pension plan.

(1) The Authority shall establish and maintain a contributory pension plan (“the Pensions Contributory Plan”) to which an employee and the Authority, shall make contributions for the benefit of the employee. (2) Where a person employed by the Authority elects to join the Pensions Contributory Plan, the Authority’s contribution to the Pensions Contributory Plan in respect of that person, shall be paid out of the Consolidated Fund to the Authority.

(3) The Minister, after consultation with the Board, shall by regulations provide for the —

(a) establishment of the Pensions Contributory Plan;

(b) rules of eligibility of the Pensions Contributory Plan;

required contributions of the Authority and the employee; amount of the pension to be paid to the employee; and

matters required to be prescribed for the establishment and grant of pensions to employees of the Authority.

**49.** Power to grant pension or gratuity in special circumstances. (1) The Authority may, with the approval of the Minister of Finance, make provision for the payment of a pension and gratuity to an employee who, although he has not completed a period of service which entitles him to a pension, is compelled to quit the Authority because of severe bodily injury, occasioned without his fault in the course of his duty.

(2) The Authority shall grant an employee under subsection (1), a gratuity not exceeding three months’ pay for every two years of service, or a pension not exceeding ten-sixtieths of his annual salary.

(3) The Authority may grant a sum of money by way of gratuity as the Authority thinks proper, not exceeding one month’s salary for each year of service, to an employee who is constrained by infirmity of mind or body to leave the employment of the Authority before the completion of the period which entitles him to a pension.

(4) Where an employee dies while in the service of the Authority, the Authority may grant —

(a) to his personal representative; or

(b) to a person designated under oath by the employee, a gratuity of an amount equal to the amount the employee would have received as pension payable at the date of his death.

## PART VI – MISCELLANEOUS

**50.** Amendment of the Second and Third Schedules.

(1) The Minister, on the recommendation of the Authority, may, by order, amend the Second Schedule.

(2) The Minister may, on the recommendation of the Authority, amend the Third Schedule by order to expand the standard health benefits.

**51.** Rights of appeal to determine grievances.

(1) A person aggrieved by —

(a) a denial of enrolment and entitlement to benefits under the Plan; or

(b) another decision of the Authority, may appeal the decision to the tribunal established under this section.

The tribunal shall consist of five persons appointed by the Minister and shall include a —

(a) medical practitioner;

(b) nurse or other health professional;

(c) counsel and attorney with at least five years' call to the Bar;

(d) member of civil society; and

(e) person from the financial sector.

(3) The members of the tribunal shall be appointed for a term of two years and may be reappointed for a further period of two years.

(4) An aggrieved person may appeal to the tribunal within twenty-one days of the decision, and the decision of the tribunal may be appealed to the Court of Appeal.

(5) The Minister may make rules governing the procedure for appeals to the tribunal and the procedure for hearings before the tribunal.

**52.** Cooperation and information sharing.

(1) The Authority may cooperate and share with the National Insurance Board and the Insurance Commission, information acquired by the Authority in the exercise of its functions under this or a written law, where the Authority —

(a) considers that such cooperation or sharing of information may be relevant to the discharge of the statutory functions of the National Insurance Board and the Insurance Commission; and

(b) has a reciprocal arrangement in writing with the National Insurance Board and the Insurance Commission for cooperation and sharing of information.

(2) The National Insurance Board shall provide the Authority with access to data required by the Authority to validate the eligibility of a beneficiary and an insured person.

(3) The Insurance Commission shall, upon request, provide the Authority with information as is necessary to enable the Authority to validate the registration of insurers to qualify as approved insurers.

(4) Notwithstanding the provisions of subsections (1), (2) and (3), the Authority shall not share confidential information concerning the medical history of a beneficiary and an insured person with another person. (5) Subsection (4) shall not apply to a disclosure of information —

(a) lawfully required or permitted by a court of competent jurisdiction for The Bahamas;

(b) of the affairs of a beneficiary and an insured person, where the consent to disclosure is given voluntarily by the beneficiary and the

insured person, or the legal guardian of the beneficiary and the insured person;

where the information disclosed does not reveal the identity or enable the identity of the approved insurer, beneficiary, and insured person to be ascertained.

(6) Information under this section shall be shared securely under the provisions of Data Protection (Privacy of Personal Information) Act (Ch. 324A).

**53.** Confidentiality.

(1) A person who encounters data or information relating to the affairs of the Authority, a beneficiary, an insured person, a provider, or an approved insurer while carrying out functions under this Act shall —

(a) treat the data or information as secret and confidential;

(b) take appropriate security measures to maintain the secrecy and confidentiality of the data or information; and

(c) not disclose the data or information without proper authorization from the Authority.

(2) A person referred to in subsection (1) shall —

(a) prevent unauthorized access to data and information described in subsection (1);

(b) prevent the alteration of the data and information; and

(c) protect the data and information against intentional or accidental loss or destruction.

(3) Subject to subsection (4), a person who contravenes subsections (1) and

(2) commits an offence.

(4) Subsection (1) shall not apply to a disclosure of data or information —

(a) lawfully required or permitted by a court of competent jurisdiction for The Bahamas;

(b) which assists the Authority in exercising a function conferred on it by this Act, or a written law;

(c) concerning the affairs of a beneficiary and an insured person where the consent of the beneficiary, insured person, or legal guardian of a beneficiary and an insured person is voluntarily given;

(d) if the information disclosed is or has been available to the public from a lawful source;

(e) if the information disclosed pertains to the person making the disclosure; or

(f) if the information is disclosed in a manner that does not enable the identity of a beneficiary, an insured person, or an approved insurer to be ascertained.

General offences.

A person commits an offence if the person —

(a) obtains benefits under this Act by making a declaration, or statement of a material nature, which he knows is false;

(b) wilfully uses or attempts to use funds of the Authority for purposes other than those authorized under this Act;

(c) agrees to an arrangement to avoid obligations under this Act;

(d) wilfully delays, assaults or obstructs an inspector, a Compliance Officer, or an agent of the Authority in the exercise of his functions under this Act;

(e) with intent to defraud, forges, falsifies or does an unlawful act to obtain a benefit or the possibility of obtaining a benefit under this Act for himself or another person at the expense of, or to the loss of, the Authority, a provider, a beneficiary and an insured person; (f) wilfully fails to pay the prescribed fees under this Act;

(g) being a provider, fails without good reason –

(i) to provide standard health benefits or other health benefits paid for in advance by the Authority; or

(ii) to account for monies received in advance for standard health benefits or other health benefits;

(h) without lawful excuse, refuses to furnish information, or to produce a document to the Authority when required to do so; or

(i) wilfully fails to comply with a requirement or to discharge a duty under this Act.

(2) A person who commits the offence under paragraph (e) of subsection (1) shall be liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding twenty-five thousand dollars.

(3) A person who commits an offence under another paragraph in subsection (1), shall be liable to the penalty stipulated in section 55.

**55.** General penalty.

*is*

Where a person commits an offence against this Act for which no other penalty specified, he is liable on summary conviction —

(a) in the case of a provider, to a fine not exceeding ten thousand dollars and where the offence continues, to a further fine of one thousand dollars for each day the offence continues; or

(b) in the case of a beneficiary and an insured person, to a fine not exceeding five thousand dollars, and where the offence continues, to a further fine of five hundred dollars for each day the offence continues.

Non-derogation.

Nothing in this Act shall derogate from —

(a) a claim for industrial benefit under the National Insurance Act (Ch. 350);

(b) a claim under a policy of insurance required under the Road Traffic Act (Ch. 220) for bodily injury arising out of a road traffic accident; and

(c) a collective agreement or existing employment contract providing sickness and health insurance with greater benefits than those provided by the standard health benefit or other health benefits. (2) For subsection (1)(a), the Authority may enter into a cost-sharing agreement with the National Insurance Board.

**57.** Audited statements of account of the Authority.

The annual statements of accounts of the Authority shall be audited by—

(a) an independent auditor appointed by the Board with the approval of the Minister; and

(b) the Auditor-General for service performance measured against the annual plan, from a date to be determined by the Minister by order.

**58.** Inspection of accounts of the Authority by the Auditor-General. Notwithstanding section 57, the Auditor-General may inspect and audit the accounts and records of the financial transactions and of the assets of the Authority.

**59.** Mid-Year and annual reports by the Board.

(1) The Board shall provide the Minister and the Financial Secretary with a mid-year report no later than one month after the end of the first six months of the financial year.

(2) The Board shall also provide the Minister and the Financial Secretary with an annual report no later than four months after the end of the financial year.

(3) The mid-year report and annual reports shall include the information specified in the Eleventh Schedule to the Public Finance Management Act 2023 (No. 4 of 2023).

(4) The annual report shall —

(a) include a copy of the statement of accounts certified by an auditor of the Authority for the financial year;

(b) set out the key activities carried out during the financial year;

(c) include a plan of the proposed objectives for the next financial year;

(d) set out the key performance indicators for the next financial year;

set out the budget of the Authority for accomplishing target activities for the next financial year;

(f) set out the priorities of the Authority for the next two financial years;

(g) contain an actuarial report prepared by the actuary;

(h) be tabled in the House of Assembly by the Minister no later than six months after the end of the financial year; and

(i) be published by the Authority on the website of the Authority, no later than one week after the annual report is tabled in the House of Assembly.

### CONSTITUTION AND PROCEDURE OF THE BOARD

**1.** Constitution of Board.

*The*

(1) Board shall consist of nine members appointed by the Minister — (a) six of whom shall consist of —

(i) one member, with experience in at least one of the following healthcare sectors –

(aa) health service delivery;

(bb) health workforce;

(cc) health information systems;

(dd) knowledge of, and access to, essential medicines;

(ee) health financing; or

(ff) health leadership or governance;

(ii) one member, with experience in at least one of the following disciplines —

(aa) finance;

(bb) law;

(cc) business administration; or

(dd) executive-level management of a business;

(iii) two members, who are patient advocates or represent civil society;

(iv) one member, selected from the nursing or allied health professions; and

(v) one member, who is a trade unionist.

(b) three members, of whom one member shall be nominated by each of the following —

(i) The Bahamas Chamber of Commerce and Employers Confederation;

(ii) The Bahamas Insurance Association;

(iii) the Medical Association of The Bahamas, and not less than forty percent of the members of the Authority shall be female and not less than forty percent of the members shall be male.

The Managing Director of the National Health Insurance Authority, the Chief Medical Officer, the Director of the National Insurance Board, the Superintendent of the Insurance Commission of The Bahamas, and the Director of Social Services shall be ex-officio members of the Board with no voting rights.

(3) The Minister shall appoint the Chairman and the Deputy Chairman from among the members appointed to the Board under subparagraph (1). (4) Before accepting membership of the Board, a proposed member shall disclose to the Minister whether he is a director, officer, or shareholder of a business that offers a service to the Plan, and if the Minister is satisfied that the member has no conflict of interest, he shall appoint the member to the Board.

(5) Upon appointment, or before discharging the duty as a member of the Board, each member shall sign a declaration and give an undertaking to adhere to the rules of procedure governing membership of the Board.

**2.** Appointment of secretary.

*The*

Board shall appoint a person to perform the functions of secretary to the Board at the remuneration and on the terms and conditions the Board may determine.

**3.** Tenure of members of the Board.

(1) Five members of the Board shall hold office for four years as specified in the instruments of appointment and may be eligible for re-appointment for an additional term of three years but shall not hold office for more than two consecutive terms.

(2) Four members of the Board shall hold office for three years as specified in the instruments of appointment and may be eligible for re-appointment for an additional term of three years but shall not hold office for more than two consecutive terms.

(3) A member may resign his office by notifying the Chairman in writing, and shall cease to be a member on the date of his resignation, and the Chairman shall inform the Minister of the resignation of the member. (4) The Chairman may resign his office by notifying the Minister in writing, and his resignation shall become effective from receipt of the resignation by the Minister.

(5) Where the Chairman or the Deputy Chairman ceases to be a member of the Board, he shall also cease to be Chairman or Deputy Chairman of the Board.

(6) The appointment, termination, death or resignation of the Chairman, Deputy Chairman or a member shall be published in the Gazette.

Vacancies.

There shall be a vacancy in the membership of the Board in the case of —

(a) the death or resignation of a member;

the termination of a member of the Board under paragraph 5; or inability to discharge the functions as a member, whether arising from infirmity of body or mind or from another cause, for a period exceeding three months.

(2) Where a vacancy exists in the membership of the Board under subparagraph (1)(a) or (b), the Chairman shall notify the Minister of the vacancy, and the Minister shall appoint another person to fill the vacancy within fourteen days of the notice.

(3) Where the Chairman determines that a vacancy exists in the membership of the Board under subparagraph (1) (c), he shall notify the Minister of the vacancy and if the Minister thinks it desirable or expedient to do so, shall revoke the appointment of the member by instrument in writing and appoint another person to fill the vacancy within fourteen days of the revocation of the appointment of the member.

**5.** Termination of membership.

*The —*

Minister shall terminate the appointment of a member of the Board, if

(a) the member is convicted of —

(i) an offence of fraud or dishonesty;

(ii) an offence of corruption, including bribery, extortion, and money laundering; or

(iii) an offence punishable with imprisonment;

(b) the conduct of the member, whether in performing his duties as a member of the Board or otherwise, brings discredit to the Board; (c) the member breaches the confidentiality provision under paragraph 10;

(d) the member is adjudged bankrupt;

(e) the member is of unsound mind, or is otherwise incapable of discharging his duties;

(f) the member has an interest in a matter before the Board, fails to disclose that interest, and participates in the deliberations of the matter before the Board in contravention of paragraph 9; (g) the member is absent from three consecutive meetings of the Board; or

(h) the member resigns as a member of the Board.

**6.** Remuneration.

There shall be paid —

(a) to a member of the Board in respect of his office, the remuneration and allowances, as the Minister may determine;

to the Chairman and the Deputy Chairman of the Board in respect of their offices, the remuneration and allowances as the Minister may determine;

to a person invited to attend meetings of the Board under paragraph 7, the remuneration and allowances, as the Board may determine.

**7.** Meetings of the Board.

(1) The Board shall meet at least eight times per year, for the transaction of the business of the Board, and a member who is unable to attend in person may participate in the meeting through electronic means approved by the Board.

(2) The Chairman shall at the request in writing of not less than one-third of the members of the Board, convene an extraordinary meeting of the Board at the time and place determined by the Chairman.

(3) The Chairman shall preside at meetings of the Board, and in the absence of the Chairman, the Deputy Chairman shall preside, and in the absence of the Chairman and Deputy Chairman, a member of the Board elected by the members present from among their number shall preside.

(4) The quorum of the Board shall consist of seven members of the Board. (5) Matters before the Board shall be decided by a majority of the members present and in the event of an equality of votes, the person presiding shall have a casting vote.

(6) The Board may invite a person who, in the opinion of the Board, has expertise in, or knowledge of, a matter before the Board relating to the functions of the Board, to attend and participate in meetings of the Board, but that person shall not be entitled to vote on a matter for decision at the meeting.

(7) The proceedings of the Board shall not be invalidated because of a vacancy among the members or a defect in the qualification or appointment of a member.

(8) Subject to this paragraph, the Board shall determine the procedure for its meetings.

**8.** Voting.

(1) Every member eligible to vote may cast a vote or abstain from voting. (2) The Chairman of the Board shall vote at the same time as the Board on all votes, but the vote of the Chairman shall be kept separate and shall only be considered in the case of a tie.

**9.** Disclosure of interest.

(1) A member of the Board who has an interest in a matter under consideration by the Board shall —

(a) disclose to the Board, in writing, the nature of the interest, and the disclosure shall form part of the record of the proceedings at which the matter is considered; and

not be present or participate in the deliberations or the decision of the Board in respect of the matter.

(2) Subparagraph (1) shall not apply if the Board is satisfied that the interest of the member concerned is not prejudicial to the principles of natural justice or public procurement requirements, and that the Board proceedings would not be open to the charge of bias, the member may take part in the proceedings, and the minutes of the meeting of the Board shall contain the fact that the member was present, the disclosure of the member, and the decision of the Board on the disclosure.

(3) If a member of the Board is the subject of discussions before the Board, that member shall withdraw from the discussions of the Board on the matter, and the absence of the member from the meeting shall be recorded in the minutes.

(4) Notwithstanding subparagraph (3), where the Board is satisfied that the presence of the member during the discussions is not prejudicial to the principles of natural justice and, that the Board proceedings would not be open to the charge of bias, then that member may take part in the Board's discussion on the matter, and the decision of the Board to allow the member to be present, and the fact of the member’s presence at the meeting, shall be recorded in the minutes.

**10.** Confidentiality.

(1) A member of the Board shall keep confidential all matters discussed at meetings unless otherwise agreed by the Board.

(2) Where an allegation of a breach of confidentiality arises, the matter shall be brought to the attention of the Minister at the earliest opportunity, and the Minister shall cause an investigation of the matter to be carried out. (3) If the Minister is satisfied after investigation, that a member of the Board is in breach of this paragraph, the member shall be removed.

**11.** Limitation of liability.

*No*

*faith*

action, prosecution, or other proceedings shall be brought or instituted against the Chairman or a member of the Board in respect of an act done in good in pursuance of the provisions of this Act.
